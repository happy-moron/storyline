Thomas sat at his kitchen table, drinking coffee.
Frank the cat sat beside him, licking his paw.
"It is Saturday," Thomas said. "No surprises today."
Then Thomas heard a loud clunk from outside.
His old car, which he had owned for twelve years, made a terrible noise.
Smoke came from the engine.
Thomas frowned. "This is bad."
Thomas called his mechanic.
The mechanic said, "Your car is dead. You need a new one."
Thomas did not like this.
He did not want a new car. He liked his old car.
Thomas went to the car dealership.
A salesman named Chad walked up to him.
"Looking for something exciting?" Chad asked.
Thomas said, "No. Just a car exactly like my old one."
Chad grinned. "How about a sports car? Red? Very fast?"
Thomas shook his head. "No. Boring. Safe. Predictable."
Chad showed Thomas a gray sedan.
It looked almost like his old car.
Thomas nodded. "This is acceptable."
He signed the papers.
He drove home, relieved.
But when Thomas parked, Frank jumped onto the hood.
Frank sniffed the car. Then he sneezed.
Thomas frowned. "What’s wrong?"
Frank meowed loudly—his "this is bad" meow.
Then Thomas smelled it.
The car had a new car smell.
Thomas hated new car smell.
It was too strong. Too unfamiliar.
Thomas drove back to the dealership.
Chad was still there.
Thomas said, "The smell is wrong."
Chad blinked. "That’s… normal?"
Thomas shook his head. "No. It must smell like my old car."
Chad sighed. "I can’t help you."
Thomas thought hard. Then he had an idea.
Thomas drove to the junkyard.
He found his old car.
He took the floor mats and the seat covers.
He put them in the new car.
Frank sniffed. Then he purred.
The car now smelled like old coffee and cat.
Thomas nodded. "Perfect."
At home, he drank coffee in peace.
Frank curled up in the passenger seat.
Thomas said, "No more surprises."
Frank meowed in agreement.

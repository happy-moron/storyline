Exactly. And to express that things did not happen in the past, we use a very important structure. In Chinese, when you want to say you "did not" do something, you use 没有 before the verb. For example, 我没有吃饭, which means I did not eat. 

en=student
Wait, I also know 不. How do I know when to use 没有 instead of 不?

en=teacher
That is a fantastic question. Generally, 不 is used for habits, the present, or the future. For example, 我不吃肉 means I do not eat meat; it is a habit. But if you want to say you didn't eat a specific meal just now, you must use 没有. So, 我没有吃午饭 means I did not eat lunch.

en=student
Okay, so 没有 is for the past action that didn't occur. What about the basic sentence structure we are learning today?

en=teacher
We are looking at the standard Subject, Verb, Object order. It is very similar to English. Subject, then the action, then the thing being acted upon. Like 我喝咖啡, I drink coffee. 

en=student
Got it. Subject, Verb, Object. So in a kitchen, it might be "The chef cooks the fish."

en=teacher
Exactly! In today's dialogue, we are going to hear a very tense moment in a busy restaurant. A stressed-out Head Chef is confronting a very slow apprentice, Xiao Ming, about some missing preparations. Listen for the use of 没有 to express things that were not completed.

en=teacher
Let's go through that intense kitchen drama line by line. First, the Chef is shouting.

zh=1

Xiao Ming! Have you prepared the fish?

en=student
"准备好" is an interesting one. "准备" means to prepare, but adding "好" after the verb indicates the action is completed successfully or done well. And the "了吗" at the end turns it into a question about a completed action.

en=teacher
Spot on. Next, Xiao Ming responds, sounding very apologetic.

zh=2
厨师，对不起。我还没有准备好。
Chef, I am sorry. I have not prepared it yet.

en=student
He says "还没有". "还" means "still" and "没有" means "not". So "还没有" means "not yet." He's not saying he *never* will, just that it hasn't happened yet.

en=teacher
Right. Then the Chef gets even more frustrated.

zh=1
你的动作太慢了！酱汁呢？你煮酱汁了吗？
You are too slow! Where is the sauce? Did you cook the sauce?

en=student
"动作" means movement or action. "太慢了" means too slow. Then he asks, "你煮酱汁了吗？" "煮" means to cook or boil, and "酱汁" is sauce. This follows our Subject, Verb, Object pattern perfectly: You (Subject) + cook (Verb) + sauce (Object).

en=teacher
Let's look at Xiao Ming's excuse.

zh=2
我没有煮酱汁。我刚才在切菜。
I did not cook the sauce. I was cutting vegetables just now.

en=student
Here is our main grammar point! "我没有煮酱汁" means "I did not cook the sauce." He uses 没有 before the verb "煮" to negate the past action. But then he says "我刚才在切菜". 

en=teacher
Yes! "刚才" means "just now." And "在" is a very useful word here. When you put "在" before a verb, it indicates that the action was in progress, like the English "-ing" form. So, "我在切菜" means "I am cutting vegetables," and "我刚才在切菜" means "I was cutting vegetables just now."

en=student
That makes sense. It explains what he was doing instead of the sauce. Now the Chef sounds like he is about to lose it.

zh=1
我们没有时间了！客人已经在等了！
We have no time left! The customers are already waiting!

en=student
"没有时间" means "no time" or "don't have time." This is a different use of 没有. Instead of negating a verb, he is negating a noun, essentially saying "time does not exist for us right now." And "客人" means customer.

en=teacher
And finally, Xiao Ming's tragic realization.

zh=2
我真的没有时间吃午饭，我太慢了。
I really did not have time to eat lunch; I am too slow.

en=student
He uses "没有时间" again, but this time it is followed by a verb, "吃午饭", which means to eat lunch. So "没有时间吃午饭" means "did not have time to eat lunch." It's a classic case of Subject, Verb, Object: I (Subject) + have no time (Verb phrase) + to eat lunch (Object/Complement).

en=teacher
It's also quite a sad ending for our intern! He's working so hard but he's just too slow.

So, today we are looking at two important grammar points. First, the reduplication of verbs. In Chinese, you can repeat a verb, like 看看 or 问问, to indicate that the action is brief, casual, or to suggest "trying" something out. 

en=student
So, instead of just saying "look," saying 看看 makes it sound more like "have a quick look" or "take a peek"?

en=teacher
Precisely. It softens the tone. Second, we are looking at how certain verbs use 在 to describe location. Instead of just saying "I am at the park," you use verbs like 站在 (to stand at), 坐在 (to sit at), or 停在 (to stop at). These verbs combine an action with a location.

en=student
So, if I want to say "I am sitting at the table," I wouldn't just say "I am at the table," I would say 我坐在桌子旁边?

en=teacher
Spot on. Now, before we get into the grammar, let's talk about the scene. You are going to hear a dialogue between a frantic tourist and a very blunt, busy street vendor. The tourist is trying to find a hotel but is currently making things a bit difficult for everyone else.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's dive into that dialogue and break it down line by line. It starts with the tourist.

zh=1
请问，请问，大酒店在哪儿？
Excuse me, excuse me, where is the Grand Hotel?

en=student
The tourist starts with 请问, which is a great one. It is a reduplication of the verb 问, meaning to ask. It's a very polite and common way to say "Excuse me, may I ask..."

en=teacher
Right. And we have 大酒店, which means Grand Hotel. 大 means big, and 酒店 means hotel. Then we have 在哪儿, which is the standard way to ask "where is...".

zh=2
你站在路中间，太危险了！快走开！
You are standing in the middle of the road, it's too dangerous! Move!

en=teacher
The vendor is not being very gentle! He says 你站在路中间. Here we see our second grammar point: 站在.

en=student
Right, 站 means to stand, and 在 means at. So 站在 means "standing at." If he had just said 你在路中间, that would mean "You are in the middle of the road," but adding the verb 站 makes it specifically about the action of standing there.

en=teacher
Exactly. And 路中间 means the middle of the road. Then he says 太危险了, which means "too dangerous."

zh=1
对不起！我刚才在看地图。我会走走看的。
Sorry! I was just looking at a map. I will walk a bit to see.

en=student
The tourist says 对不起, sorry. Then, 我刚才在看地图. 刚才 means "just now" or "a moment ago." And 看 is to look.

en=teacher
Now, listen to this part: 我会走走看的. This uses the reduplication we talked about. 走 means to walk. By saying 走走, he is saying "walk a little" or "walk a bit." And adding 看 at the end, like 走走看, is a very common structure that means "try walking to see" or "walk a bit and see."

en=student
So it is like "I will walk around and see if I can find it." That's so useful for when you aren't sure of a direction.

zh=2
你去那个公园，坐在长椅上，等一下出租车。
Go to that park, sit on the bench, and wait for a taxi.

en=teacher
The vendor gives some directions. 你去那个公园, "Go to that park." Then he says 坐在长椅上.

en=student
Here is another one! 坐 means to sit, and 在 means at. So 坐在 means "sitting at" or "sitting on." And 长椅 means bench.

en=teacher
Correct. And then he says 等一下出租车. 等 is to wait, and 一下 is a common way to indicate a short duration of time, like "a little bit."

zh=1
好的，谢谢你！
Okay, thank you!

zh=2
没关系。出租车会停在路边。
No problem. The taxi will stop at the roadside.

en=teacher
The vendor finishes with 没关系, which means "it doesn't matter" or "no problem." And finally, he says 出租车会停在路边.

en=student
Here we go again with the verb plus 在. 停 means to stop, and 在 means at. So 停在 means "to stop at." And 路边 means the roadside.

en=teacher
Perfect. You can use 停在 for cars, buses, or even people stopping at a specific spot.

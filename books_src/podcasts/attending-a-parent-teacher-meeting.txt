en=student
Okay, "什么 + 都" or "也". That is "everything" or "anything," right? Like, "I eat everything"?

en=teacher
Exactly. For example, 我什么都吃 (I eat everything). Or, if you want to say you don't eat anything, you can say 我什么都不吃 (I don't eat anything). You can also use 也. 我什么也不吃 and 我什么都不吃 mean the same thing. The "也" is often used in negative sentences to add emphasis.

en=student
And the second one? "别...了"?

en=teacher
"别 + Verb + 了" is used to tell someone to stop an ongoing action or to not do something anymore. Like, 别说话了 (Stop talking) or 别哭了 (Stop crying). It often implies that the action is already happening and needs to cease.

en=student
Got it. So, "什么都" is about "everything" or "nothing," and "别...了" is a way to say "stop it."

en=teacher
Perfect. In today's dialogue, you are going to hear a very tense meeting. Ms. Zhang, a school teacher, is meeting with Mrs. Li, a parent, to discuss her son, Xiao Ming. It is not going to be a very friendly conversation.

Exactly. The "了" at the end is important here. It signals a change in state or a command to end an ongoing behavior. It's like saying, "Stop doing that thing you are currently doing." And "纪律" means discipline.

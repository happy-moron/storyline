Exactly. Today, we are looking at two very useful grammar points to help you express frustration or describe significant differences. First, we have the "every time" structure using 每次 and 都. The pattern is 每次 plus Event One, then you must include 都 before Event Two. For example, 每次我喝咖啡，都会觉得很开心 (Every time I drink coffee, I feel very happy).

en=student
So, 每次 sets the recurring time, and 都 emphasizes that the action happens every single time. It is like saying "every time... always...".

en=teacher
Precisely. Second, we are learning how to say "much more" when making comparisons. Instead of just saying something is more than something else, you can use the structure Noun One plus 比 plus Noun 2 plus Adjective plus 了 plus 多. For example, 今天比昨天热多了 (Today is much hotter than yesterday).

en=student
That sounds perfect for when you want to add some emphasis. If I say 他比我高, it means "He is taller than me," but if I say 他比我高多了, it means "He is much taller than me."

en=teacher
Spot on. In our dialogue today, you are going to hear Lili, a babysitter who is currently having a very, very stressful evening. She is on the phone with her friend, Xiao Ming, venting about how difficult these two children are.

en=student
"每次" (měi cì) is every time. And we see "都" used here, though she said "就" (jiù) to add emphasis to the immediacy. Actually, James, can I use 每次 without 都?

en=teacher
You can, but it sounds incomplete in most spoken contexts. Adding 都 makes it clear that this is a repetitive, predictable pattern. In this sentence, "吵架" (chǎo jià) means to argue or quarrel.

en=student
Got it. And then she says:

zh=1
这根本不是在玩！每次我给他们做饭，他们都会弄脏地板。

en=teacher
Notice "根本" (gēn běn) here. It means "at all" or "simply" when used with a negative. It adds that dramatic flair Lili is feeling. And here is "每次" again. "每次我给他们做饭" (Every time I cook for them), "他们都会" (they always will), "弄脏" (nòng zāng - to make dirty) "地板" (dì bǎn - floor).

en=student

zh=1
难多了！这两个孩子比上个星期那个孩子难对付多了！
Much harder! These two kids are much more difficult to deal with than that kid from last week!

en=teacher
This is a perfect example of our comparison grammar. "难多了" (nán duō le) means "much more difficult." 

en=student
And the long sentence: "这两个孩子比上个星期那个孩子难对付多了". Let's dissect that. "这两个孩子" is the subject. "比" introduces the comparison. "上个星期那个孩子" is the object being compared—the kid from last week. Then "难对付" is the adjective meaning "difficult to deal with," and we finish with "多了" to say "much more."

en=teacher
Exactly. If she just said "这两个孩子比那个孩子难对付," she would just be saying "These two kids are more difficult to deal with than that kid." Adding "多了" at the end provides that massive emphasis on the scale of the difference.

en=student
So, "比...多了" is the way to go when you really want to emphasize that the difference is huge.

en=teacher
Correct. Finally, Xiao Ming tries to comfort her.

zh=2
别太累了，加油！
Don't get too tired, hang in there!

en=student
"加油" (jiā yóu) is the classic "come on" or "keep going." And "别太累了" is a kind way to say "don't overwork yourself."

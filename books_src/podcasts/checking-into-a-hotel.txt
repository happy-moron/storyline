Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am James, and today we are looking at some very practical structures you will need when traveling, specifically when checking into a hotel.

en=student
I definitely need these. There is nothing more stressful than arriving at a hotel after a long flight and having a problem with your reservation.

en=teacher
Exactly. To help you navigate that, we are covering two key grammar points. First, the structure of Verb plus Number plus Measure Word. In English, we just say "book a room" or "see three reviews." But in Chinese, when you specify a quantity, you almost always need a measure word between the number and the noun. For example, 订了一个房间, which means "booked one room," or 看了三个好评, which means "saw three good reviews."

en=student
So the measure word is like the glue that holds the number and the noun together. If I want to say "two rooms," I can't just say "两个房间" without a verb, right? I need the action first.

en=teacher
Right, though the "Verb + Number + MW" pattern specifically focuses on how you describe the action performed on a certain number of things. Second, we are learning how to use 不要 to give negative commands or advice. You simply put 不要 before a verb. For example, 不要担心, which means "don't worry," or 不要生气, which means "don't get angry."

en=student
That sounds straightforward. So if a receptionist tells me I shouldn't be worried, they would say "不要担心"?

en=teacher
Perfect. Today's dialogue features a bit of tension. You are going to hear a guest who is quite frustrated because his hotel reservation seems to be missing. He is talking to a hotel receptionist.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's break down this dialogue line by line. We start with the guest.

zh=2
你好，我预订了一个房间。
Hello, I booked one room.

en=student
"预订了一个房间" (yù dìng le yī gè fáng jiān). This is a perfect example of our first grammar point. 预订 is the verb "to book," 一个 is "one" plus the measure word "gè," and 房间 is "room."

en=teacher
Exactly. Without that "个," the sentence would sound very broken. Then the receptionist responds.

zh=1
对不起，先生。我没看到您的预订。
I'm sorry, sir. I don't see your reservation.

en=student
"对不起" is "I'm sorry." "没看到" means "didn't see." And "预订" here is being used as a noun, meaning "reservation."

en=teacher
Right. Now the guest is getting a bit impatient.

zh=2
不要跟我开玩笑！我订了两个晚上！
Don't joke with me! I booked two nights!

en=student
"不要跟我开玩笑!" That's a very useful phrase. "不要" is "don't," "跟我" means "with me," and "开玩笑" means "to joke." It's a very natural way to say "Don't kid me!" 

en=teacher
And then he uses our first grammar point again: "订了两个晚上." 订 is the verb "to book," 二个 is "two" plus the measure word "gè," and 晚上 is "evening" or "night." 

en=student
So even though "nights" feels different in English, the Chinese structure remains Verb plus Number plus Measure Word plus the time period.

en=teacher
You've got it. Now, the receptionist tries to calm him down.

zh=1
请不要着急，我再检查一下。
Please don't worry, I will check again.

en=student
"不要着急" (bù yào zháo jí). "着急" means "to worry" or "to be anxious." So "不要着急" is "don't worry" or "don't be anxious." It's a very common phrase in service industries.

en=teacher
And "再检查一下" (zài jiǎn chá yī xià) means "check again." "再" means "again," and "一下" makes the action "a little bit" or "briefly," making the tone more polite. Now, the guest tries to persuade her.

zh=2
我刚才看了三个好评，说这里非常好。
I just saw three good reviews saying this place is very good.

en=student
"刚才" means "just now." "看了三个好评" is our grammar point again. 看了 is "saw/read," 三个 is "three" plus the measure word "gè," and 好评 is "good reviews." 

en=teacher
Exactly. "好" is "good" and "评" comes from "评价," which means "to evaluate" or "to comment." Finally, the receptionist finds the mistake.

zh=1
找到了！您预订了一个大房间。
Found it! You booked one large room.

en=student
"找到了" (zhǎo dào le) is "found it." And "一个大房间" is "one large room." 

en=teacher
Notice the order: "一个" is the number and measure word, then the adjective "大" (big), and then the noun "房间" (room). The measure word still sits right next to the number.

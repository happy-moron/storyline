First, we have the adverb 再, which is used to express "again" for an action that has not happened yet, or a future intention. For example, we can say 我明天再来, which means I will come again tomorrow. It is different from 又, which we use for things that have already happened.

en=student

Precisely! You've got it. The second point is using 都 to emphasize "already." When you use the structure 都 plus a time or amount, plus 了, it adds an emotional weight—often a sense of "it is already [too] late" or "it is already [so much]." For example, 都十点了, which means "It's already ten o'clock!" (with a hint of "it's getting late!").

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's look at that dialogue and break it down line by line. It starts with the daughter calling her mother.

zh=2
妈，你怎么还没看到我回信息？

en=student
"你怎么..." is a common way to ask "why" or "how come." And "还没" means "not yet." She's asking why the message hasn't been seen yet.

zh=1

It's already three in the afternoon! Where on earth are you?

en=teacher
Here is our first grammar point! "都下午三点了" uses the "都...了" structure. As we mentioned, the "都" adds emphasis. She isn't just saying it's three o'clock; she's saying "It's already three!" implying that the time has passed and the daughter should have been here by now.

en=student
And "到底" (dào dǐ) means "on earth" or "exactly," right? Like, "Where on earth are you?" It adds that sense of frustration.

en=teacher
Exactly. Now, let's listen to the daughter's response.

"在路上" is "on the way." And "忙" is "busy." Simple and effective.

zh=1

en=teacher
The mother is bringing up the past here. "去年也是这样" means "last year was also like this." It's that classic family observation, isn't it?

en=student
Very real. Now, let's look at the next line where we hear the first use of our "again" grammar.

zh=2

en=teacher
"再聊" (zài liáo). She is saying "we will talk again." This is the future "again" because the conversation hasn't happened yet. If they had already finished talking and she wanted to say "let's talk again" regarding a past event, she would use "又."

en=student
So, "再" is for the things that are yet to come. Like, "I will do it again tomorrow."

You've mastered the difference. If she had already waited and was complaining, she might say "I already waited for twenty minutes" using "又." But here, it's a future intention.

Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am James, and today we are talking about something most of us dread: cleaning the house.

en=student

en=teacher
Exactly. Today we are focusing on two very important grammar points. First, the verb 可以, which we use to express permission or possibility. For example, 你可以坐 (You may sit) or 我可以去吗？ (May I go?).

en=student
Right, so it is about whether you have permission to do something. And the second one is 要, which means "to want" or "to be going to." For example, 我要吃饭 (I want to eat) or 我要学习 (I am going to study).

en=student
Is there a difference between 要 and 想? I always get them mixed up.

en=teacher
That is a great question. 想 is more like "would like to"—it is a feeling or a desire. 要 is much stronger. It implies intention, necessity, or that something is definitely going to happen. If you say 我要睡觉, you are saying "I am going to sleep" or "I really need to sleep," not just "I would like to sleep."

en=student
So 要 is much more direct. Got it. What about the vocabulary for today?

en=teacher
We will use words like 打扫 (to clean), 房间 (room), 垃圾 (trash), and 客人 (guest).

en=student
And the theme?

en=teacher
In this episode, you are going to hear a conversation between a couple, Mei and Li. They are living together and they are feeling the stress of a messy apartment because they have guests coming over for dinner very soon. It is a bit of a tense situation!

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's look at that dialogue, and break it down line by line. It starts with Mei, speaker number one.

zh=1
这个房间太乱了！我们要打扫一下。
This room is too messy! We need to clean it up a bit.

en=student
"这个房间太乱了" is a great way to express frustration. 太...了 is the structure for "too [adjective]." And "打扫" means to clean. But James, look at "我们要打扫一下". Why did she say "要"?

en=teacher
In this context, "要" expresses necessity or a strong intention. She isn't just thinking about it; she is saying "We must clean" or "We are going to clean." Also, notice the "一下" after "打扫". Adding "一下" after a verb makes the action sound casual, like "doing something for a bit."

zh=2
我现在要休息。
I want to rest right now.

en=student
Li is being very blunt! "我现在要休息" means "I want to rest right now." Using "要" here shows his firm intention to do nothing.

zh=1
你不想打扫吗？今天晚上我们要客人来吃饭。
Don't you want to clean? We have guests coming for dinner tonight.

en=teacher
Mei uses the negative "不想" here to ask, "Don't you want to...". Then she explains why: "我们要客人来吃饭." Here, "要" acts as "to have" or "is going to happen." We have guests coming for dinner.

zh=2
那我可以休息一会儿吗？
Then can I rest for a little while?

en=student
Aha! Here is our first use of "可以"! "我可以休息一会儿吗？" She is asking for permission to rest.

en=teacher
Exactly. "可以...吗?" is the standard way to ask "May I...?" or "Can I...?" in a way that asks for permission.

zh=1
不可以。你看这些垃圾，我可以扔了吗？
No. 不可以 means "No" or "You may not." And then she asks, "我可以扔了吗？" Can I throw it away?

en=student
So "可以" is "can/may," and "不可以" is "cannot/may not." That is very straightforward. And "垃圾" is trash, right?

en=teacher
Yes, "垃圾" is trash or rubbish.

zh=2
可以。但我想要买一些新的桌布。
Yes. But I want to buy some new tablecloths.

en=student
Li gives permission: "可以." But then he uses "想要" (xiang yao). Is that different from just "要"?

en=teacher
Yes! As we mentioned in the intro, "想要" is a combination that softens the "want." It is very similar to "would like to." It sounds a bit more polite or expresses a desire rather than a command or a hard necessity.

zh=1
我们现在没钱。我们要先把地板擦干净。
We don't have money now. We need to wipe the floor clean first.

en=student
Mei shuts him down. "我们现在没钱" (We have no money right now). And then she uses "要" again: "我们要先把地板擦干净."

en=teacher
Exactly. She is saying "We need to wipe the floor clean first." "擦" means to wipe, and "干净" means clean. She is setting the priority. She is using "要" to express what they must do before they can worry about anything else.

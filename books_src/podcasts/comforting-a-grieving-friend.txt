en=teacher
Exactly. To help you navigate these conversations, we are looking at two very important grammar points. First, we will look at how to express close possession without using the particle, de. In English, we always say "my mother" or "your friend," but in Chinese, when the relationship is very close, like family or close friends, we often drop the, de. So instead of saying 我的妈妈, you can simply say 我妈妈.

en=student
Okay, so it's a way to show closeness. It makes the relationship sound more intimate.

en=teacher
Precisely. The second grammar point is using 了, or, to express completion. This is the structure where you have a subject, a verb, and then 了 to indicate that an action has been completed. For example, 我吃了 (I ate) or 我听到了 (I heard).

en=student
So, the 了 acts as a marker that the action is done. It's not exactly like the English past tense, but it functions similarly to show completion.

en=teacher
That is a great way to think about it. Now, let's look at our six key vocabulary words for today.

en=teacher

en=teacher

en=teacher
Fifth, we have 陪伴, (third tone and fourth tone), meaning to accompany... 陪伴.

en=teacher

en=teacher
That's a great question, Ryan. While you can technically drop "de" with parents, in this context, he is referring to "your mother," which is a person outside of his immediate circle, so using "你的" is very natural. Also, look at "我已经...了" (wǒ yǐ jīng... le). "已经" means "already," and when you use it with "了" at the end, it strongly emphasizes that the action is finished. He has already completed the phone call.

zh=1

en=student
Wait, Ellen, he said "没吃饭" (méi chī fàn). I remember you saying "了" is for completion. If he hasn't eaten, shouldn't there be a "了"?

en=teacher
That is a common point of confusion! In Chinese, when we negate an action to say it did not happen in the past, we use "没" (méi) instead of "不" (bù), and we do not use "了" at the end of the sentence. So, "我没吃饭" means "I didn't eat" or "I haven't eaten." If you said "我没吃了," that would actually be grammatically incorrect in this context.

en=student
Ah, I see. "没" replaces the need for "了" when you are negating the completion.

en=teacher
Yes! "陪伴" is the noun or the more formal verb for "to accompany," but in daily conversation, we often just use the single character "陪" followed by the person we are accompanying. It's much more natural.

en=teacher
It can be difficult to find the right words when emotions are high. Today, we are going to learn two grammar points that will help you sound more natural and empathetic. First, we will look at how to express close possession without using the particle "de". In Chinese, when you are talking about very close relationships—like family or even a very close pet—you can often drop the "de" and just use a Pronoun followed by a Noun. For example, instead of saying "我的妈妈" (my mother), you can simply say "我妈妈".

en=student
Oh, so it's like saying "my mom" instead of "my mother" in English, but it implies a much closer level of intimacy?

en=teacher
Exactly. It sounds more natural and warmer. Second, we are looking at using "le" to express the completion of an action or a change of state. When you add "了" after a verb, it often indicates that the action has been completed. For example, "我吃了" means "I ate." But it can also indicate a change of state, like "他来了" meaning "he has arrived" or "他来了" meaning "he has come."

en=student
I see. So "le" can be about the action being finished, or it can be about things changing. Like, if I say "我变了", it means "I have changed."

en=teacher
Precisely. Now, before we get into our dialogue, let's go over our six key vocabulary words. 

en=teacher

en=teacher

en=teacher
We have 陪, (second tone), meaning to accompany or to stay with... 陪.

en=teacher

It can be scary! And because of that drama, we are going to focus on two very essential grammar points. First, the standard negation using 不, which is used with verbs and adjectives to say "not" or "do not." For example, 我不吃 (I do not eat) or 我不累 (I am not tired).

en=student
Right, so it is for present or habitual actions, and for adjectives. But I always get confused with 没. Is there a difference?

en=teacher
Yes! That is a great question, Ryan. You use 没 to negate past actions or to say you "do not have" something. But for describing a current state, like "I am not tired," or a preference, like "I do not want," you must use 不.

en=student
Got it. And the second grammar point is the adverb 都, which means "all" or "both."

en=teacher
Exactly. 都 is used to indicate that something applies to every member of a group. It always comes after the subject and before the verb or adjective. For example, 我们都是学生 (We are all students) or 他们都很开心 (They are all very happy).

en=student
So if I'm talking about a group of friends, I would say 他们都不喜欢 (They all do not like it).

en=teacher
Precisely! You are using both! Now, before we get into the drama, let's look at our key vocabulary.

en=teacher

en=teacher
We have 疼, (second tone), meaning painful or to hurt... 疼。

en=teacher
We have 舒服, (first tone and neutral tone), meaning comfortable or well... 舒服。

en=teacher

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's look at that dialogue and break it down line by line. We start with Li Hua's reaction to the pain.

zh=1
哎呀！好疼啊！
Ouch! It hurts so much!

en=student
"哎呀" is such a classic exclamation. It is like "Oh my" or "Ouch." And "好疼" is "so painful."

en=teacher
Right. Then Zhang Wei rushes in.

zh=2
你怎么了？你在流血！
What happened to you? You are bleeding!

en=student
"你怎么了?" is the standard way to ask "What's wrong?" or "What happened to you?" And "流血" means to bleed.

en=teacher
Now we get into the core of our lesson. Listen to Li Hua explain the accident.

zh=1
我刮胡子，不小心割到了。
I was shaving, and I accidentally cut myself.

en=student
There is our first "不"! "不小心" (not careful) means accidentally. And "刮胡子" is the verb we learned earlier.

en=teacher
Exactly. "割到" means to cut or to have been cut. Then Zhang Wei reacts with some frustration.

zh=2
你怎么这么不小心？所有的剃须刀都这么锋利吗？
Why are you so careless? Are all razors this sharp?

en=student
"怎么这么..." is a great phrase for "Why so..." or "How come so..." It sounds very natural. And here is our second grammar point! "所有的...都..."

en=teacher
Yes! "所有的" means "all of" or "every," and it is followed by the noun "剃须刀" (razor). Then we use "都" before the adjective "锋利" (sharp) to emphasize that it applies to all of them.

en=student
So "所有的学生都..." would be "all of the students..." That makes perfect sense. Now, Li Hua sounds a bit paranoid.

zh=1
我不知道。我不觉得这把刀正常。
I do not know. I do not think this razor is normal.

en=student
"我不觉得" uses "不" to negate the verb "觉得," which means to feel or to think.

en=teacher
Right. He doesn't think it's normal. Zhang Wei tries to help.

zh=2
别动。我也要去拿点药。
Do not move. I will also go get some medicine.

en=student
"别动" is a command, "don't move." And "我也要" uses "也" for "also." He is saying he will also go to get medicine.

en=teacher
And finally, the climax of the pain and the resolution.

zh=1
快点！我真的不舒服！
Hurry! I really do not feel well!

zh=2
没关系，我们以后都要小心一点。
It is okay, we both must be careful in the future.

en=student
"不舒服" means "not comfortable," but in a health context, it means "to feel unwell." 

en=teacher
And "没关系" is the most important phrase for any learner—"it's okay" or "it doesn't matter." Finally, "我们以后都要小心一点" uses "都" to say that *both* of them (or all of them) need to be careful in the future.

en=student
So, "都" emphasizes the collective nature of the action. "We all/both must be careful."

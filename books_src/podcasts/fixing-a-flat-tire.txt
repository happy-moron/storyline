INTRO

en=teacher
Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are dealing with a situation that can turn any road trip into a nightmare: a flat tire.

en=student
Oh man, that sounds stressful. I really don't want to deal with that while driving through the countryside.

en=teacher
Precisely. And when things go wrong, you need the right grammar to express your frustration or to reassure your friends. Today, we are focusing on two very important structures. First, the standard negation using 不. You use 不 before a verb or an adjective to negate a present or habitual action, or to express a desire. For example, 我不吃 (I don't eat) or 我不累 (I am not tired).

en=student
Okay, so it's like saying "do not" or "am not." But wait, if I'm talking about something that already happened, like "I didn't eat," do I still use 不?

en=teacher
That is a great question, Ryan. For completed actions, we usually use 没有, but today we are sticking to the core usage of 不 for descriptions and current states. Second, we are learning about the "all" adverb, 都. You place 都 before a verb or an adjective to indicate that something applies to everyone or everything in a group. For example, 我们都喜欢 (We all like it) or 他们都很高 (They are all very tall).

en=student
So if I say 我们都不, I am saying "none of us" or "we all do not"?

en=teacher
Exactly. You are combining negation and "all" to say that the negative applies to the entire group. Before we hear the dialogue, let's look at our six key vocabulary words.

en=teacher
We have 轮胎, (second tone, first tone), meaning tire... 轮胎。

en=teacher
We have 坏, (fourth tone), meaning broken or spoiled... 坏。

en=teacher
We have 换, (fourth tone), meaning to change or to replace... 换。

en=teacher
We have 工具, (first tone, fourth tone), meaning tools... 工具。

en=teacher
We have 着急, (second tone, second tone), meaning to worry or to be anxious... 着急。

en=teacher
We have 备用, (fourth tone, fourth tone), meaning spare or reserve... 备用。

en=student
Got it. And what is the scenario?

en=teacher
In this episode, you are going to hear two friends, Li Hua and Xiao Mei, who are on a road trip. Suddenly, the car starts shaking, and they realize they have a problem on a very lonely road.

DIALOGUE

zh=1
糟糕！轮胎坏了！这里一个车也没有！
Oh no! The tire is broken! There isn't a single car here!

zh=2
别着急，我们都有备用轮胎。
Don't worry, we both have a spare tire.

zh=1
我不记得带了工具！我们真的倒霉透了。
I don't remember bringing the tools! We are truly out of luck.

zh=2
后备箱里都有工具，别担心。
There are tools in the trunk, don't worry.

zh=1
我真的不会换。我觉得这太难了。
I really don't know how to change it. I think this is too difficult.

zh=2
我们慢慢来，我们都可以学会。
Let's take it slow, we can both learn.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's dive into that dialogue and break it down line by line. We start with Li Hua, who sounds quite panicked.

zh=1
糟糕！轮胎坏了！这里一个车也没有！
Oh no! The tire is broken! There isn't a single car here!

en=student
"糟糕" (zāo gāo) is such a perfect, dramatic way to say "oh no" or "what a mess." And "轮胎坏了" is a classic structure. "轮胎" is the tire, and "坏了" is broken.

en=teacher
Right. That "了" at the end of "坏了" is important; it indicates a change of state. The tire was fine, and now it is broken. Then he says "这里一个车也没有." This is a more advanced way to say "there are no cars," using "一...也/都 不..." to mean "not even one."

zh=2
别着急，我们都有备用轮胎。
Don't worry, we both have a spare tire.

en=student
Xiao Mei is much calmer. She says "别着急" (bié zháo jí). "别" means don't, and "着急" means to worry.

en=teacher
Exactly. Then comes our grammar point: "我们都有." "我们" is we, and "都" means all or both. So, "we both have." She's reassuring him.

zh=1
我不记得带了工具！我们真的倒霉透了。
I don't remember bringing the tools! We are truly out of luck.

en=student
Here is our first use of "不" for negation. "我不记得" means "I don't remember."

en=teacher
Correct. And "倒霉透了" (dǎo méi tòu le) is a very colorful way to say "to be extremely unlucky." "透" (tòu) here acts like "to the extreme."

zh=2
后备箱里都有工具，别担心。
There are tools in the trunk, don't worry.

en=student
Xiao Mei is staying positive. She says "都有" (dōu yǒu) again. "There are all the tools in the trunk."

en=teacher
Yes, she is emphasizing that the tools are indeed there.

zh=1
我真的不会换。我觉得这太难了。
I really don't know how to change it. I think this is too difficult.

en=student
"我真的不会换" (wǒ zhēn de bù huì huàn). This is a great example of "不会." "会" means to know how to do something, so "不会" means "don't know how to" or "cannot."

en=teacher
Precisely. And "我觉得这太难了" (wǒ jué de zhè tài nán le) uses "觉得" to express an opinion, "I feel/think," and "太...了" to mean "too..."

zh=2
我们慢慢来，我们都可以学会。
Let's take it slow, we can both learn.

en=student
"我们都可以学会" (wǒ men dōu kě yǐ xué huì). "都" again! "We can both learn."

en=teacher
It's a very encouraging way to end the conversation. "学会" means to learn a skill.

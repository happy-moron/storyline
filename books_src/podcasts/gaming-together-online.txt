Exactly. Today, we are mastering two ways to be much more specific. First, we have the possessive particle 的. This is the glue that connects a person to their things. You simply take the person, add 的, and then the object. For example, 我的电脑, which means my computer, or 你的耳机, which means your headset.

en=student
So it's like adding an apostrophe-s in English, but it always goes after the person. 

en=teacher
Spot on. Second, we are looking at the question particle 呢. This is a fantastic, short way to ask "What about...?" or "Where is...?" if something is missing. For instance, if you are looking for your keys, you can just say 我的钥匙呢？ which means "Where are my keys?" or "What about my keys?".

en=student

en=teacher
Great question. 哪里 is used for "where" in a general sense, like "Where are you going?". But 呢 is much more natural when you are looking for something that you expect to be there, or when you are asking "What about...?" to keep a conversation going.

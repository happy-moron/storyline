BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's break down that dialogue. I will say the lines again, slowly and clearly.

zh=1
你好！请问你在等朋友吗？
Hello! May I ask, are you waiting for a friend?

en=student
Okay, 你好 is a standard hello. But 请问 sounds a lot more formal. Is that like saying "may I ask" in English?

en=teacher
Exactly, Ryan. It is a very polite way to start a question. And then we have 你在等朋友吗. Here, 在 is used before the verb 等, which means to wait. This tells us the waiting is happening right now.

en=student
And the response was 没有，我没在等朋友。 Why did the customer say 没有 first?

en=teacher
In Chinese, when someone asks a yes or no question using 吗, you often answer with 没有 to say "no" if the question is about an action or possession. And then the customer says 我没在等朋友. Here, we use 没 to negate the progressive action.

en=student
So, if I am not eating, I say 我没在吃.

en=teacher
Perfect! Now, let's look at the next part.

zh=2

en=student
There it is! 正在! Is that the one that is more emphatic?

en=teacher
Yes. 我正在找理发店 means "I am right in the middle of looking for a barber shop." And the word for barber shop is 理发店. Let's break that down. 理 is part of the word for hair, 发 is also hair, and 店 means shop.

en=student
理发店. Got it. And then the barber says 这里就是理发店. That's a very direct "this is it," isn't it?

上次 means "last time." It is a very useful word. And 什么时候 means "when."

zh=2

I did not have a haircut last month.

en=student
This is interesting. The customer is talking about the past here. They said 我上个月没有剪头发. Is that where we use 没有 to negate a past action?

en=teacher
Exactly. When we want to say we did not do something in the past, we use 没有. So, "I didn't cut hair last month."

en=student

Yes! 剪 is to cut, and 短 is short. 剪短 means to cut something short.

You have a sharp ear, Ryan! That is a complement of degree, but let's keep it simple for today. It's basically describing *how* the hair is being cut. In this case, "cut it the same as this photo."

en=teacher
Let's break down that intense conversation. It starts with Mei being quite direct.

And then she says "都不干净" (dōu bù gān jìng). Does "都" mean "all"?

en=teacher
Yes! "都" means all or both. So "都不干净" means "none of them are clean" or "all are not clean."

en=teacher
Exactly. 不太 (bú tài) plus the adjective 忙 (máng), meaning busy. It's much more common in daily conversation than just saying "I am not busy."

en=student
And he says "一会儿" (yī huì r). That sounds like "in a little while," right?

en=teacher

en=student
"每次都" means "every time." And here we see "和" again with "碗和杯子"—bowls and cups.

en=teacher
That's right. And notice Mei uses "非常脏" (fēicháng zāng). "非常" means "very" or "extremely." It's much stronger than the "不太" we just learned.

zh=2
你能不能别总是这么生气？
Can you stop being so angry all the time?

en=student
"总是" (zǒng shì) means always. She is asking him why he's always so angry.

en=teacher
Wait, she is actually asking "Why shouldn't I be angry?" in the next line because Li is trying to deflect.

zh=1

en=student
"为什么" (wèi shén me) is why. And "一点都不干净" (yī diǎn dōu bù gān jìng)... is that similar to "不太"?

It's stronger! "不太干净" means "not very clean," but "一点都不干净" means "not clean at all." It’s a much more emphatic way to complain!

en=student

Your attitude is not very good, and I also feel very unhappy!

"态度" (tài dù) is attitude. "不太好" means not very good. And "不开心" (bù kāi xīn) is unhappy.

en=teacher
Exactly. She's using "不太" to describe both his attitude and her own feelings of unhappiness.

en=teacher
Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are tackling a classic point of confusion for almost every learner: the difference between the two ways to say "can" in Chinese.

en=student
Oh, man. This is the one that always trips me up. Is it 会 or is it 能? It feels like they mean the same thing.

en=teacher
It is a very common struggle! But there is a very clear distinction. Today, we are learning the difference between 会, which expresses a learned skill, and 能, which expresses physical ability or situational possibility.

en=student
Okay, so if I say "I can swim" because I took lessons and learned the technique, I should use 会?

en=teacher
Exactly. 我会游泳. But, if you have a broken arm and you physically cannot swim right now, even though you know how, you would use 能. 我不能游泳.

en=student
That makes total sense. So 会 is about "knowing how to," and 能 is about "being capable of" or "having the circumstances to."

en=teacher
Precisely. One is about skill, the other is about capacity or opportunity.

en=student
Can I use them interchangeably at all?

en=teacher
In a few very specific cases involving physical senses or very basic human functions, they can overlap, but for ninety percent of your life, the distinction is crucial. If you tell someone "我会走路" you are saying "I know the skill of walking," which sounds like you are a toddler who just learned to stand up!

en=student
Haha, okay, I will avoid that. So, what is the scenario for today?

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's look at that dialogue, and break it down line by line. It starts with Dawei checking on his friend.

zh=1
莉娜，你还好吗？你还能走吗？
Li Na, are you okay? Can you still walk?

en=student
"你还好吗？" is a standard "Are you okay?". But then he asks, "你还能走吗？". He used 能 there.

en=teacher
Yes. He uses 能 because he is asking about her physical capacity in this moment. Can her legs keep moving, or has she reached her limit?

en=student
And Li Na's response is quite blunt!

zh=2
我非常累。我不能再走了。
I am extremely tired. I cannot walk anymore.

en=teacher
She says "我不能再走了." Again, she uses 能. It's not that she doesn't know how to walk—she's not a baby—it's that her current physical state, being extremely tired, makes walking impossible right now.

en=student
So, if she said "我不会走路," she would be saying "I don't know how to walk."

en=teacher
Exactly! That would be a very weird thing to say on a hike. Now, Dawei tries to offer a solution.

zh=1
别担心，我们可以在这里休息一会儿。
Don't worry, we can rest here for a little while.

en=student
He uses 能 again here: "我们可以..." which means "we can" or "it is possible for us to."

en=teacher
Right. This is the third use of 能: situational possibility. It is possible for them to rest there. Now, Li Na asks about a skill.

zh=2
你会看这张地图吗？
Do you know how to read this map?

en=student
There it is! She uses 会. She is asking if Dawei possesses the learned skill of map reading.

en=teacher
Correct. And Dawei's answer highlights the difference perfectly.

zh=1
我不太会看地图，但我会用手机导航。
I don't know how to read maps very well, but I can use mobile navigation.

en=student
"我不太会看地图." He uses 不太 which means "not very" or "not too," followed by 会. He's saying he hasn't mastered the skill of reading a physical map. But then he says "我会用手机导航."

en=teacher
Yes, using a GPS or mobile navigation is a learned skill, so he uses 会. He knows how to operate the software.

en=student
And finally, Li Na tries to find a silver lining for herself.

zh=2
太好了，至少我会拍照！
Great, at least I can take photos!

en=teacher
She uses 会 here because photography is a skill she has acquired. Even if she can't hike well, she's got the photo-taking skill covered!

en=student
So, just to recap: 会 for skills like 拍照 or 看地图, and 能 for physical limits like 不能走路 or for possibilities like 可以在这里休息.

en=teacher
Spot on.

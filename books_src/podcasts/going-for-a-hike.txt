INTRO

en=teacher
Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are heading outdoors to tackle a topic that is both physically demanding and grammatically interesting: going for a hike.

en=student
I love hiking, but I definitely struggle when I don't know how to read a map! It can be a bit overwhelming.

en=teacher
Exactly! And that is perfect for our lesson, because we are learning the difference between two very important ways to express ability in Chinese.

en=student
You mean the difference between 能 and 会? I always get those mixed up when I am trying to say what I can or cannot do.

en=teacher
It is a common hurdle. Here is the trick. We use 会 to express a learned skill. If you have studied something, like a language, or learned a craft, like cooking, you use 会. For example, 我会中文, meaning I can speak Chinese because I learned it.

en=student
And 能 is for something else?

en=teacher
Yes. 能 is used for physical ability or situational possibility. For example, if you are asking if someone is physically able to walk, you would use 能. Or, if you are asking if a situation allows for something, like "Can I park here?" or "Can I come to the party?", you use 能.

en=student
So, if I say "I can swim" because I took lessons, I use 会. But if I am in the middle of the ocean and someone asks if I am able to swim to the shore right now, I use 能?

en=teacher
That is a perfect way to put it! Now, before we head up the mountain, let's look at our six key vocabulary words for today.

en=teacher
We have 爬山, (second tone and first tone), meaning to climb mountains or to hike... 爬山.

en=teacher
We have 准备, (third tone and fourth tone), meaning to prepare... 准备.

en=teacher
We have 累, (fourth tone), meaning tired... 累.

en=teacher
We have 地图, (fourth tone and second tone), meaning map... 地图.

en=teacher
We have 方向, (first tone and fourth tone), meaning direction... 方向.

en=teacher
We have 路, (fourth tone), meaning road or path... 路.

en=student
Got it. Now, what is our dialogue about?

en=teacher
In this scene, you are going on a hike with an experienced guide. Your partner is a bit overwhelmed and feeling exhausted, and you will hear them discuss their abilities and the path ahead.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's look at that dialogue and break it down line by line. We start with the guide.

zh=1
快走吧！我们要去爬山了！
Hurry up! We are going hiking!

en=student
"快走吧" is a very common way to say "hurry up." And we see our first vocabulary word: 爬山. 

en=teacher
Correct. Then the novice hiker responds.

zh=2
我觉得有点累。
I feel a bit tired.

en=student
"我觉得" means "I feel" or "I think." And there is our third vocab word, 累.

en=teacher
Exactly. And then they add something very important using our grammar point.

zh=2
而且我真的不会看地图，这太难了。
And I really don't know how to read a map, it's too hard.

en=student
Wait, Ellen, why did they say 我不会看地图 instead of 我不能看地图?

en=teacher
That is the most important question of the day! Remember, they said "I don't know how to read a map." Reading a map is a skill that you have to learn. Therefore, you use 会. If they said 我不能看地图, it might mean they are physically unable to look at it, perhaps because they are wearing dark sunglasses!

en=student
That makes sense! It's the difference between "I don't know how" and "I am unable to."

en=teacher
Exactly. Now, let's look at the guide's reassurance.

zh=1
没关系，我会看地图。
It's okay, I know how to read maps.

en=student
Here is 会 again, because the guide has the learned skill.

zh=1
你看，路就在那边。
Look, the path is right over there.

en=teacher
And we have our fifth vocab word, 路, meaning road or path.

en=student
Now the novice asks a question about their capacity.

zh=2
我们现在还能走多远？
How much further can we walk now?

en=teacher
This is a great use of 能. They are asking about their physical capability and the possibility of the situation.

en=student
And the guide is very encouraging here.

zh=1
别担心，只要你准备好了，我们一定能到山顶。
Don't worry, as long as you are prepared, we can definitely reach the summit.

en=student
We have 准备 here, meaning to prepare. And then we have 能 again. "We can definitely reach the summit." This isn't a learned skill; it's a possibility based on their preparation and physical ability.

en=teacher
Spot on. Finally, the novice asks for permission.

zh=2
太好了，那我能休息十分钟吗？
That's great, then can I rest for ten minutes?

en=student
Is this the same "can" as in "I can swim"?

en=teacher
Yes! In this context, 能 is used to ask for permission. "Am I allowed to rest?"

en=student
So, 会 is for skills, and 能 is for capability, possibility, or permission.

en=teacher
You've got it. And the guide, being a professional, finishes the conversation.

zh=1
当然可以！
Of course!

That is exactly why we are covering this today. We are going to learn two very important structures. First, the degree complement, which is the "Verb plus de" structure. We use this to describe the manner or the degree of an action. For example, if you want to say "He speaks very fast," you would say 他说得很快. The "de" acts as a bridge between the verb and the description of how that verb was performed.

en=student
So, it is not just saying "He is fast," it is "He speaks *in a fast manner*."

en=teacher
Exactly. It describes the state or the result of the action. Second, we are looking at the potential complement, specifically the "verb plus bu dong" structure. This is used to express that you are unable to understand something through listening or reading. For example, 听不懂 means "cannot understand by listening," and 看不懂 means "cannot understand by reading."

en=student
"头疼" is a headache. But then he says "疼得厉害". That "de" is the degree complement!

en=teacher
Yes! "疼" is the verb "to ache/pain," and "厉害" means "severe" or "terrible." By putting "de" in the middle, he is saying the degree to which it hurts is "severe." It is a very common way to intensify a sensation.

en=student
So, if I want to say "I am very happy," I can't use "de" there, right?

en=teacher
Correct. "de" follows a verb or an adjective to describe the *action's* manner. For "happy," you would just say 我很高兴. But for an action like "to cry" or "to laugh," you could say "He cried terribly" using the complement.

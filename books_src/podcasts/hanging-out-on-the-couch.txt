en=teacher
In a way, yes! We are focusing on two very practical grammar points. First, we have the use of 怎么, which means "how" or "why." When you place it directly before a verb, it can mean "how to do something," but it can also mean "how come" or "why," often expressing surprise or confusion. For example, 你怎么不说话? (Why aren't you speaking?) or 你怎么去? (How do you go?).

en=student
So, it is a bit more emotive than just saying "why"?

en=teacher
Exactly. It carries a sense of "how is this possible?" or "why is it that...". Second, we are looking at the structure for indicating location during an action. This is Subj. + 在 + Place + Verb. In English, we say "I eat in the kitchen," but in Chinese, we use 在 before the verb to set the stage. For example, 我在学校学习 (I study at school) or 我在家里睡觉 (I sleep at home).

en=student
That makes sense. You establish the location before you describe the action.

en=teacher
Precisely. Before we get to our scene, let's look at our key vocabulary for today.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's dive into that dialogue and break it down line by line. It starts with the first speaker, who sounds a bit confused.

zh=1
你怎么在那儿坐着？
Why are you sitting there?

en=student
"你怎么..." There it is! "How come you..." or "Why are you..." Ryan here, asking for clarification. And "在那儿" means "at that place" or "there," right?

en=teacher
Yes. "在" is the preposition for location, and "那儿" is there. The verb is "坐着", where "坐" is to sit, and "着" indicates a continuous state. So, "Why are you in the state of sitting there?"

zh=2
我在这儿看电视。
I am watching television here.

en=student
"我在这儿看电视" follows the grammar we just talked about. Subject, "在", location, then verb. "I am at here watching TV."

en=teacher
Exactly. Note how "在" comes before the location "这儿" (here).

zh=1
你怎么不学习？你已经在这儿躺了两个小时了！
Why aren't you studying? You have already been lying here for two hours!

en=student
This is a great example of "怎么不" (zěn me bù). It sounds like "Why don't you...?" 

en=teacher
Spot on. "怎么" plus a negative like "不" is a very common way to ask "Why aren't you doing X?" It often implies that the speaker thinks you *should* be doing something else. And then we have "你已经在这儿躺了两个小时了". 

en=student
Wait, "躺了两个小时". I see "躺" (to lie down) and then "两个小时" (two hours). Is "了" used for duration here?

en=teacher
Very observant! Yes, "了" indicates the completion or the duration of an action. "He has been lying there for two hours."

zh=2
我在这儿玩手机。我觉得很累。
I am playing on my phone here. I feel very tired.

en=student
"玩手机" (wán shǒu jī) is a phrase we use all the time now. "Playing phone." And "觉得" (jué de) means "to feel."

en=teacher
Yes, "我觉得很累" is "I feel very tired." A very useful phrase for when you've spent too much time on the couch!

zh=1
你怎么不起来？我们出去吃点东西。
Why don't you get up? Let's go out to eat something.

en=student
"你怎么不起来？" "起来" means to get up. "Why don't you get up?" And "出去吃点东西" is a very natural way to say "go out to eat a little something."

zh=2
我不想动。我在这儿休息一会儿。
I don't want to move. I am resting here for a little while.

en=student
"我不想动" (wǒ bù xiǎng dòng) is "I don't want to move." That is the ultimate lazy person's sentence. And then the last line: "我在这儿休息一会儿". 

en=teacher
"一会儿" (yī huì er) is a great phrase to know. It means "a little while" or "for a bit." So, "I am resting here for a little while."

en=student
So, to recap the location rule, it's always "Subject + 在 + Place + Action." It's like you have to set the stage before the actors start moving.

en=teacher
That is a perfect metaphor, Ryan. You establish the location first, then you describe what is happening in that location.

en=student
And "怎么" is my go-to for when I'm surprised. If my friend is sleeping at noon, I won't ask "Why are you sleeping?" using the formal "为什么", I'll say "你怎么在睡觉?" (How come you are sleeping?).

en=teacher
Exactly. It adds that layer of "Wait, what?" to your question.

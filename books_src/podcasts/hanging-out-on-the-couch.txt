en=teacher
Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are looking at how to express persistence and emphasis. 

en=student
That sounds important. Are we talking about being stubborn? Or just talking about things that keep happening?

en=teacher
Both, actually. Today, we are mastering two very common structures. First, the adverb 还, which can mean "still" or "yet." You use it before a verb or an adjective to indicate that a situation is continuing. For example, 我还在吃, which means I am still eating. Or, if you add a negative, you can say 我还没吃, meaning I haven't eaten yet.

en=student
Okay, so it's like "still" when it's affirmative, and "not yet" when it's negative. But wait, isn't that the same as 也?

en=teacher
That is a common point of confusion! 也 means "also" or "too." If you say 我也是, you are saying "I am also/too." But if you say 我还在, you are saying "I am still." They serve very different purposes.

en=student
Got it. So, what is the second one?

en=teacher
The second one is 就是. While 这 means "this" and 是 means "is," when you put them together before a verb, it acts as an intensifier. It's used for emphasis, often to mean "simply," "just," or "exactly." For example, 我就是不想去, which means "I simply don't want to go." It adds a layer of certainty or even a bit of attitude.

en=student
So it's like adding "just" to a sentence to show you're being firm about your stance.

en=teacher
Precisely. In our dialogue today, we're going to see these used in a very relatable setting: being incredibly lazy.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's look at that dialogue, and break it down line by line. It starts with Xiao Ming, who is clearly losing his patience.

zh=2
你怎么还在沙发上看电视？
Why are you still watching television on the couch?

en=student
"你怎么..." is a classic way to start a question, like "How come...?" or "Why...?" But the key part here is "还在".

en=teacher
Exactly. "还在" means "still doing" something. You have the verb "看" (to watch) and "电视" (television). Together, "还在看电视" means "still watching television."

zh=1
我还没看够呢。
I haven't seen enough yet.

en=student
"还没" again! That's the "not yet" version of "还". And "看够" sounds like "watch enough."

en=teacher
Yes. "看够" means "to watch until satisfied" or "to see enough." Adding "呢" at the end adds a bit of a soft, casual tone to her statement, almost like a whine.

zh=2
你就是不想起床！
You simply don't want to get up!

en=student
There it is! Our second grammar point. "你就是不想..." That "就是" is doing a lot of heavy lifting there.

en=teacher
It is. Instead of just saying "You don't want to get up," Xiao Ming is saying "You *simply* don't want to get up." He's emphasizing her stubbornness. It makes the sentence much more pointed.

zh=1
我觉得我还没休息够。
I feel like I haven't rested enough yet.

en=student
"我觉得" is "I feel" or "I think." And "休息够" is "to rest enough." So "还没休息够" is "haven't rested enough yet."

en=teacher
Correct. Notice how "还" is being used with "没" to create that "not yet" meaning.

zh=3
他就是想偷懒。
He just wants to slack off.

en=student
Lao Li enters the chat! "他就是想..." He's using "就是" to agree with Xiao Ming's sentiment.

en=teacher
Right. And "偷懒" is a great verb here. It literally means "to steal laziness." It's the perfect word for slacking off or being lazy.

zh=2
我们今天还要去超市呢。
We still have to go to the supermarket today.

en=student
"还要" here. Does this mean "still have to" or "also need to"?

en=teacher
In this context, it's "still have to." It implies that there is a list of things to do, and the supermarket is one of them that is being neglected.

zh=1
我还想再睡一会儿。
I want to sleep for a little while longer.

en=student
"一会儿" is a very common phrase for "a little while." And "还想" means "still want to."

zh=3
他就是个懒虫。
He is just a slacker.

en=student
"懒虫"? That sounds like "lazy bug."

en=teacher
It is! "懒虫" is a very common, colloquial way to call someone a "slacker" or a "lazybones." It's not necessarily a heavy insult, but it's definitely teasing.

zh=2
别说了，我们还要准备作业呢。
Stop talking, we still have to prepare for homework.

en=student
"别说了" is "stop talking" or "forget it." And we have "准备" which we mentioned earlier—to prepare.

zh=1
可是我就是不想动。
But I simply don't want to move.

en=student
And the grand finale. "我就是不想动." She's being very firm!

en=teacher
"不想动" means "don't want to move." By adding "就是", she's essentially saying, "I just don't feel like moving, and there's nothing you can do about it." It's the ultimate lazy person's response.

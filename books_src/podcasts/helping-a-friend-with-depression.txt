Exactly. To navigate this, we are learning two vital grammar structures. First, the use of 在 or 正在 before a verb to express an action in progress. It is the equivalent of adding "ing" to an English verb. For example, 我在吃饭 (I am eating) or 他正在看书 (He is currently reading).

en=student
And the second one is the negation of "to have" or a completed action using 没有.

en=teacher
Right. In Chinese, we don't use 不 to negate "to have" or to say something didn't happen in the past. We use 没有. For example, 我没有钱 (I don't have money) or 我没有去学校 (I didn't go to school).

en=student
So, if I want to say "I am not eating" as in "I'm not currently in the middle of an action," I'd say 我不在吃饭?

en=teacher
Actually, it's even simpler. You can say 我没在吃饭 to mean "I am not [currently] eating." But if you want to say "I didn't eat," you say 我没有吃饭. It's a common point of confusion!

en=student
Got it. One is for "not doing it right now" and the other is for "didn't do it" or "don't have it."

"你在哪里?" is a classic "where are you?". But the next part, "我在找你," is interesting. That's the "in progress" grammar we talked about. "找" is to look for, so "我在找你" is "I am looking for you."

en=teacher
Correct. And "一直" (yī zhí) means "continuously" or "all along," which adds to the feeling of her frustration.

"我在睡觉." There's that "在" again. "睡" is to sleep, so "我在睡觉" means "I am sleeping." But then he says "我没精神."

en=teacher
Yes. Here, "没" is a short form of "没有." He is saying he "doesn't have" energy or spirit. It's not that he's "not energetic" in a general sense, but he currently lacks that "精神" or mental energy.

zh=1
你已经睡了很长时间了。你到底在做什么？你今天有没有吃饭？
You have been sleeping for a very long time. What on earth are you doing? Have you eaten anything today?

en=student
"你到底在做什么?" The "到底" (dào dǐ) really adds emphasis, like "What on earth..." or "What exactly...". And then the question "你今天有没有吃饭?"

en=teacher
That's a very common way to ask a "yes/no" question in Chinese. Instead of just a question particle, you use the "A not A" pattern: "有没有" (have or not have). So, "Have you eaten or not eaten?"

zh=2

en=student
"我没有吃饭." This is a perfect example of "没有" for a past action. He isn't saying he "is not eating" right now, he's saying he "did not eat" earlier.

en=teacher
Exactly. And "没胃口" is a very common phrase. "胃" means stomach, and "口" means mouth. Together, "胃口" is appetite. "我没胃口" means "I have no appetite."

zh=1
别这样。我真的很担心你。你觉得怎么样？
Don't be like this. I am really worried about you. How are you feeling?

"担心" is to worry. "我真的很担心你" is "I really worry about you." And then "你觉得怎么样?" which we saw in the intro as a way to ask "how are you feeling?"

en=teacher
Right. "觉得" is to feel.

zh=2

I feel very tired. I don't want to do anything.

en=student
"我觉得很累." "累" is to be tired. And then this last part: "我什么都不想做." "什么" means "what," but in this structure, "什么都" means "anything" or "everything." So "I don't want to do anything at all."

en=teacher

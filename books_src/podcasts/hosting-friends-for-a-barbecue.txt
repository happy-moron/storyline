INTRO

en=teacher
Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am James, and today we are going to clear up some confusion that almost every learner faces at some point. We are talking about the three different ways to say "can" in Chinese: 会, 能, and 可以.

en=student
Oh man, the "can" dilemma. I always struggle with that. In English, I just say "I can cook" or "Can I go to the bathroom?" but in Chinese, it feels like there are three different rules.

en=teacher
It does feel that way! But there is a logic to it. First, we have 会 (huì). This is used for learned skills. If you had to study or practice to know how to do it, use 会. For example, 我会说中文 (I can speak Chinese) or 我会游泳 (I can swim).

en=student
Okay, so that's for skills you've acquired. What about 能 (néng)?

en=teacher
能 is more about capability, physical ability, or circumstances. For example, 我能走 (I can walk, perhaps after an injury) or 我今天能去 (I can go today, meaning I am able to or it's possible for me to go). And then we have 可以 (kěyǐ). This is primarily used for permission or to suggest that something is possible or okay. Like, 我可以进来吗？ (Can/May I come in?)

en=student
Got it. 会 is for "know how to," 能 is for "be able to," and 可以 is for "may I" or "it is okay."

en=teacher
Exactly. And to make things even more interesting, we are also going to tackle the difference between 二 (èr) and 两 (liǎng). Both mean "two," but you can't just swap them.

en=student
Right, I know you use 两 when you're counting things with a measure word, like "two people," but 二 is used for numbers and math.

en=teacher
Precisely. Today's episode is all about hosting a barbecue for friends. You'll hear a host, Xiao Ming, preparing for the party, and his friends, Xiao Li and Xiao Zhang, checking in. Listen for how they use these "can" words and how they count their drinks and food.

Let's break down that conversation. We start with Xiao Ming asking Xiao Li a question.

en=student
"你今天能来" (nǐ jīntiān néng lái). Here, we use 能 (néng) because he is asking about Xiao Li's ability or possibility to attend. It's about whether his schedule allows it.

en=teacher
Right. And "参加烧烤" (cānjiā shāokǎo) means to attend/participate in a barbecue. Now, look at Xiao Li's response.

zh=2
当然可以！我会烤牛排，你别担心。
Of course! I know how to grill steak, don't worry.

en=student
"当然可以" (dāngrán kěyǐ) is a very common way to say "Of course!" or "Certainly!" as a way of giving permission or agreement. But then he says "我会烤牛排" (wǒ huì kǎo niúpái).

en=teacher
That's the key! He uses 会 (huì) because grilling steak is a skill he has learned. He knows the technique. "牛排" (niúpái) is steak.

en=student
And then Xiao Zhang jumps in.

zh=3
我也能来！我可以带两瓶可乐吗？
I can come too! Can I bring two bottles of cola?

en=teacher
Xiao Zhang uses 能 (néng) to say he is able to come. Then he asks "我可以带...吗？" (wǒ kěyǐ dài... ma?). He's asking for permission to bring something.

en=student
And notice the "two bottles." He says "两瓶可乐" (liǎng píng kělè).

en=teacher
Yes! Since he is counting the bottles, he uses 两 (liǎng) instead of 二 (èr). He's using the measure word "瓶" (píng) for bottles. "可乐" (kělè) is cola.

en=student
Then Xiao Ming responds about the date and the food.

zh=1
没问题。烧烤在二月二号，我已经准备好了两块牛排。
No problem. The barbecue is on February second, and I have already prepared two pieces of steak.

en=teacher
This is a perfect example of the two types of "two." He says "二月二号" (èr yuè èr hào), which is February second. When you say dates or numbers, you use 二 (èr).

en=student
But when he talks about the steak, he says "两块牛排" (liǎng kuài niúpái).

en=teacher
Exactly. "块" (kuài) is the measure word for pieces of meat, and because we are counting the pieces, we must use 两 (liǎng).

太好了！我真的很会吃！
Great! I really know how to eat!

en=teacher
"我会吃" (wǒ huì chī) is a humorous way to say someone is a big eater or knows how to enjoy food. It's like saying "I am skilled at eating!"

en=teacher
He asks for permission with 可以 (kěyǐ), and Xiao Ming gives permission with "可以" (kěyǐ). But then, look at the very last part. "我只能吃两个汉堡" (wǒ zhǐ néng chī liǎng gè hànbǎo).

en=student
"只能" (zhǐ néng) means "only can." He's talking about his physical limit or capacity. He can't eat more than two.

en=teacher
And again, "两个" (liǎng gè) uses 两 (liǎng) because "个" (gè) is the measure word for the hamburgers.

en=student
So, just to recap the vocab: 烧烤 (shāokǎo) is barbecue, 牛排 (niúpái) is steak, 可乐 (kělè) is cola, 蔬菜 (shūcài) is vegetables, and 汉堡 (hànbǎo) is a hamburger.

en=teacher
And the grammar: 会 for skills, 能 for ability or limits, and 可以 for permission. And for "two," 二 is for numbers and dates, while 两 is for counting things with measure words.

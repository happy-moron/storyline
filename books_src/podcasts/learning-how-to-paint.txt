That is why we are here, Ryan. Today, we are learning two very important grammar points to help you describe your work. First, we are looking at the simple sentence structure of Noun plus 很 plus Adjective. In Chinese, when you want to say something "is" an adjective, you do not use the verb "to be." Instead, you use the adverb 很. For example, you would say 颜色很深, which means "the color is dark," rather than using the word for "is."

en=student
So, if I want to say "the painting is beautiful," I don't say the painting is beautiful using "is," I use "很"?

en=teacher
Exactly. If you just say "the painting is beautiful" without the "很," it sounds like you are comparing it to something else. The "很" acts as a necessary link in a descriptive sentence. Second, we are learning how to use 一些 to express "some." You simply place it before the noun you are talking about. For example, 一些水, meaning "some water."

en=student
That sounds simple enough. "Some colors," "some water," "some paint."

en=teacher
Precisely. Before we get to our dramatic studio scene, let's look at our six key vocabulary words.

INTRO

en=teacher
Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are exploring a very versatile little word: 了.

en=student
Oh boy, 了. That one always gets me. Is it the "completed action" one or the "change of state" one?

en=teacher
It can be both, but today we are focusing on its role in signaling a change. We are looking at two specific ways to use it. First, using 不 or 没 plus a verb phrase followed by 了 to express "not anymore." For example, 我不喝咖啡了 means I don't drink coffee anymore. I used to, but now I have stopped.

en=student
So it's not just saying "I am not drinking coffee," but rather, "I'm done with coffee" or "I've stopped drinking coffee."

en=teacher
Exactly. Second, we are looking at how 了 signals a new situation. This is when something changes from one state to another. For example, 下雨了 means it has started to rain. It wasn't raining, but now it is. Or, 我有男朋友了 means I have a boyfriend now. It's a change in my relationship status.

en=student
Got it. So the first one is about stopping an action, and the second is about a new state of being. 

en=teacher
Perfect. Now, let's look at our key vocabulary for today. 

en=teacher
We have 紧张, (third tone, third tone), meaning to be nervous... 紧张.

en=teacher
We have 准备, (third tone, fourth tone), meaning to prepare... 准备.

en=teacher
We have 欢迎, (first tone, second tone), meaning to welcome... 欢迎.

en=teacher
We have 奇怪, (second tone, fourth tone), meaning strange or weird... 奇怪.

en=teacher
We have 明白, (second tone, second tone), meaning to understand or to realize... 明白.

en=teacher
And we have 介绍, (fourth tone, fourth tone), meaning to introduce... 介绍.

en=teacher
Let's break down this tense dinner conversation, line by line. We start with Chen, trying to calm his girlfriend down.

en=student
"别紧张" is "don't be nervous." We just learned that. But what about "我们要见我父母了"? Is that the "completed action" 了?

en=teacher
Good question. In this context, it's the "new situation" 了. It implies that the state of "not meeting the parents" is about to change. The meeting is imminent. It's a shift in the current situation.

en=student
Okay, that makes sense. Then Lin responds.

zh=2
我真的很紧张。我准备了很多。
I am really nervous. I prepared a lot.

en=teacher
She is definitely feeling the pressure. She uses "准备" (zhǔn bèi), which we noted means to prepare. 

en=student

zh=3

You're here? Welcome, welcome!

en=teacher
Yes, "欢迎" (huān yíng) is a very common way to welcome someone. And notice "你们来了" (nǐ men lái le). The "了" here indicates a change of state: they weren't here, and now they are.

en=student
Then things get a bit awkward. The mother notices the silence.

zh=3

Xiao Lin, why aren't you talking anymore?

en=student
Wait, here is one of our grammar points! "你怎么不说话了" uses the "not anymore" structure.

en=teacher
Exactly. "不说话" means "not to talk." But by adding "了" at the end, it becomes "not talking anymore." It implies that she was talking earlier, but has now stopped.

en=student
Right. And Lin apologizes.

en=teacher

Mmm, this tastes delicious! I understand your intentions now.

en=student
"我明白你的心意了." This is another "new situation" 了, isn't it?

en=teacher
Yes! Before, the mother might have been skeptical or unsure about Lin. But after tasting the food, her understanding has changed. She "has realized" or "now understands" the girl's good intentions. It's a realization that changes her perspective.

en=student
And then Chen tries to lighten the mood with a bit of humor.

zh=1
妈，你现在不觉得她奇怪了吧？
Mom, you don't think she is strange anymore, do you?

en=teacher
"不...了" again! "不觉得...了" means "don't think... anymore." He's asking if her "strangeness" has disappeared in her mother's eyes.

en=teacher
"姑娘" (gū niang) is a lovely way to say girl or young woman. A successful meeting!

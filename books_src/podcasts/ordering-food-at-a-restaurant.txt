INTRO

en=teacher
Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are stepping into a very busy, slightly stressful setting: a popular restaurant during the dinner rush.

en=student
I love food, but I definitely don't love waiting in a long line!

en=teacher
Exactly. And because it is busy, we are going to learn two grammar points that are incredibly useful for navigating any space or managing your time. First, the structure 从...到... which means "from... to...". You can use this for time, like "from five to six," or for space and range, like "from the first page to the last page."

en=student
Okay, so it covers both time and physical distance. That's very versatile. What about the second one?

en=teacher
The second one is using 往, which means "toward." You use it before a direction word to indicate the direction of movement. For example, 往左走, which means "walk to the left."

en=student
Wait, is 往 the same as saying "to" or "towards" in English? And can I use it for everything?

en=teacher
It's used specifically for the direction of movement. For example, you wouldn't say you are going "toward" a person in the same way you go "toward" a door, though you could. It is most common with verbs of motion like walking, running, or driving.

en=student
Got it. So, in our dialogue, we'll hear about how long the wait is and how to find a table.

en=teacher
Exactly. You're going to hear a conversation between a waiter, a very impatient customer, and her calmer partner. They are trying to find a place to eat in a crowded restaurant.

en=student

en=teacher

en=student

Correct. "打算" is to plan, and "吃完" means to finish eating. Now, let's look at the partner trying to calm her down.

zh=3

en=student
"别生气" is a great phrase, "don't be angry." And here we have "往里面走走".

en=teacher
Yes, "往里面" means "toward the inside." And you noticed "走走"? That is a common way in Chinese to double a verb. It makes the action sound casual or suggests "doing something for a little bit" or "trying something out." So "走走看" is like "walk a bit and see."

en=student
That's a helpful nuance. Then the waiter comes back to guide them.

zh=1

"这边" means "this side" or "this way." So "往这边走" is "walk toward this way." Very simple and very useful.

en=student
Then we get back to the impatient customer, and she uses the second version of our grammar point.

From the first page to the last page of the menu, I can't even bear to look at it.

en=student
"从第一页到最后一页" - "from the first page to the last page." That's using "从...到..." for a physical range, right?

en=teacher
Exactly! Instead of time, she is describing the extent of the menu. From page one to page last.

en=student
And "看不下去" is a very expressive way to say "can't bear to keep doing something" or "can't face it." She's so frustrated she can't even look at the menu!

Haha, yes. And finally, the partner tries to be positive.

The taste here is very special, you will definitely like it.

en=student
"味道" is taste or flavor. "特别" is special. 

en=teacher
Yes. And "一定" means "definitely." So, "you will definitely like it." It's a great way to encourage someone.

en=student

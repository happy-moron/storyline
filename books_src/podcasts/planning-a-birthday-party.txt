I feel that in my soul, James. Planning a party is stressful enough without having to worry if you've finished the tasks or if you've bought enough gifts for every single person!

en=teacher
That is actually perfect, because those are exactly the grammar points we are covering today. First, we are looking at the result complement "wan". In Chinese, if you want to say you have "finished" doing an action, you often take the verb and add "wan" immediately after it. For example, if you finish eating, you say "chi wan". If you finish reading a book, you say "kan wan".

en=student
So, "wan" acts like a little label that tells us the action reached its conclusion? It's not a separate sentence, it's just stuck onto the verb.

en=teacher
Exactly. It's a result complement. You aren't just "eating," you have "eaten to completion." Now, our second point is using "mei" to mean "every." In English, we say "every friend" or "every cake." In Chinese, you can't just put "mei" and a noun together. You must sandwich a measure word in the middle. So, instead of just "mei peng you," you must say "mei ge peng you."

en=student
So, it's "every" + "measure word" + "noun." Like "mei ge ren" for "every person" or "mei ge ji" for "every chicken." 

en=teacher
Spot on. And to make it even more emphatic, like "every single one," we can use "mei yi ge." In our dialogue today, you're going to hear two friends, Mei and Li, trying to get everything ready for a surprise birthday party. Mei is feeling the pressure, and Li is trying to help her stay calm. They'll be talking about what they have finished and what they still need to do for every guest.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's go back to the dialogue and take it apart, line by line. Let's start with Mei's first sentence.

zh=1
我们还没准备完生日派对呢！
We haven't finished preparing for the birthday party yet!

en=student
"准备完" (zhǔnbèi wán). Here we see our result complement in action. "准备" means to prepare, and "完" means to finish. So "准备完" means "finished preparing." And I noticed she added "ne" at the end. Is that for emphasis?

en=teacher
Yes, "ne" here adds a bit of tone, showing that the situation is ongoing or emphasizing the current state of affairs. Now, look at Li's response.

zh=2
别担心。你买完蛋糕了吗？
Don't worry. Have you finished buying the cake?

en=student
"别担心" (bié dānxīn) is a very useful phrase, "don't worry." Then we have "买完" (mǎi wán). "买" means to buy, and "完" is there to ask if the action is completed. And the "le" at the end of "买完了吗" turns it into a question about a completed action.

en=teacher
Correct. Now, let's look at Mei's next line, where she's getting a bit more detailed.

zh=1
蛋糕还没买完。我们还需要买很多礼物。
The cake isn't finished being bought yet. We also need to buy many gifts.

en=student
She uses "还没买完" (hái méi mǎi wán). "还没" means "not yet." So "not yet finished buying." And "礼物" (lǐwù) is gift. This is a great word to know.

en=teacher
It's essential. Now, let's look at how Li uses our "every" grammar point.

zh=2
没关系。每一个朋友都需要一份小礼物，对吧？
It's okay. Every friend needs a small gift, right?

en=student
"每一个朋友" (měi yī gè péngyǒu). Here, "mei" is "every," "yi" is "one," and "ge" is the measure word for people or general objects. Together, "mei yi ge" makes it sound like "every single one." And "一份" (yī fèn) is the measure word for gifts or sets of things. 

en=teacher
Right. Now, let's look at Mei's next task.

zh=1
对。而且我们要把房间装饰完。
Yes. And we need to finish decorating the room.

en=student
"装饰" (zhuāngshì) is to decorate. And again, "装饰完" (zhuāngshì wán)—finish decorating. She also uses "把" (bǎ) here. Is that because the object is being acted upon?

en=teacher
Exactly! The "bǎ" construction is used when you are doing something to an object that changes its state. In this case, the room is being changed by the act of decorating. Now, let's hear Li's final encouraging words.

zh=2
没问题。每一件装饰品都会让派对很漂亮。
No problem. Every single decoration will make the party very beautiful.

en=student
"每一件装饰品" (měi yī jiàn zhuāngshìpǐn). This is interesting. "件" (jiàn) is the measure word for items or pieces, and "装饰品" (zhuāngshìpǐn) means decorations. So "every single piece of decoration." He's really being optimistic!

en=teacher
He is. "漂亮" (piàoliang) is to be beautiful or pretty. So, through this dialogue, we've seen how "wan" completes our verbs and how "mei" requires that middle measure word to talk about "every" item or person.

Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are tackling a topic that every parent knows is both a major milestone and a major headache: potty training a child.

en=teacher
It definitely can be! And because it is such a hands-on process, it is the perfect way to practice asking about completed actions. Today, we are looking at two very important question structures.

en=student
Let me guess, the ones from the HSK one curriculum?

en=teacher
Exactly. First, we have the basic yes-no question using 吗. You simply add 吗 to the end of a sentence to turn a statement into a question. For example, 你是学生吗？ meaning "Are you a student?".

en=student
And the second one is the one that always trips me up: adding 了 and 吗 to a verb?

en=teacher
Precisely. When you use 吗 to ask a general question, you are often asking about a habit or a state. But when you use Verb plus 了 plus 吗, you are asking if an action has been completed or if it has happened. For example, 你吃饭吗？ means "Do you eat rice?" or "Do you eat meals?". But if you say 你吃饭了吗？ it means "Have you eaten?"—asking about a specific, completed meal.

en=student
Ah, so the 了 changes it from "do you" to "did you" or "have you". That makes a huge difference!

en=teacher
It makes all the difference! Before we hear the drama unfold in our dialogue, let us look at our six key vocabulary words.

en=teacher
We have 尿尿, (fourth tone, fourth tone), meaning to pee... 尿尿.

en=teacher
We have 拉屎, (first tone, third tone), meaning to poop... 拉屎.

en=teacher
We have 尿布, (fourth tone, fourth tone), meaning a diaper... 尿布.

en=teacher
We have 马桶, (third tone, third tone), meaning a toilet... 马桶.

en=teacher
We have 进步, (fourth tone, fourth tone), meaning progress... 进步.

en=teacher
We have 困难, (fourth tone, kun, second tone, nan), meaning difficult or difficulty... 困难.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let us look at that dialogue, and break it down line by line. We start with the mother.

zh=1
宝宝睡好了吗？
Did the baby sleep well?

en=student
"宝宝" (bǎo bao) is baby. "睡好了吗" (shuì hǎo le ma). I noticed you used "好" there. Is that just "good"?

en=teacher
In this context, it is a resultative complement. "睡" means to sleep, and "好" implies the action was completed successfully or satisfactorily. So, "睡好了" means "slept well" or "finished sleeping well." And we have our target grammar: "了" for completion and "吗" for the question.

zh=2
睡了。但是，他用马桶尿尿了吗？
He slept. But, did he pee in the toilet?

en=student
"睡了" (shuì le). She is confirming the action is done. And "但是" (dàn shì) means "but."

en=teacher
Right. Then we have "他用马桶尿尿了吗？". "用" (yòng) means to use. "马桶" (mǎ tǒng) is the toilet we mentioned earlier. And "尿尿" is the verb for peeing.

en=student
So the whole phrase "用马桶尿尿" is "to use the toilet to pee."

en=teacher
Exactly. Now, let us look at the mother's response.

zh=1
没有，他还在用尿布。他今天拉屎了吗？
No, he is still using diapers. Did he poop today?

en=student
"没有" (méi yǒu) is the standard way to say "no" for completed actions. And then we have "还在" (hái zài). Does that mean "still"?

en=teacher
Yes, "还" (hái) means still, and "在" (zài) indicates an ongoing state or action. "他在用尿布" means "He is using diapers." And then we get to the blunt part: "他今天拉屎了吗？". "今天" (jīn tiān) is today. And "拉屎" (lā shǐ) is the verb for pooping.

en=student
That is definitely a real-life term! You probably wouldn't use that in a formal meeting, but at home? Definitely.

en=teacher
Absolutely! Real language is often blunt. Let's look at the nanny's exciting news.

zh=2
拉了！他今天进步很大！
He did! He made great progress today!

en=student
"拉了" (lā le). Just the verb and the completion marker. Simple and effective. And "进步很大" (jìn bù hěn dà). "进步" is progress, and "很大" is very big. So, "progress is very big."

en=teacher
Yes. In Chinese, instead of saying "he progressed a lot," you often say "his progress is very big." Now, the mother's relief.

zh=1
太好了。如厕训练难吗？
That's great. Is potty training difficult?

en=student
"太好了" (tài hǎo le) is a classic "that's great!" or "wonderful!" Then she asks "如厕训练难吗？". "如厕训练" (rú cè xùn liàn) must be the formal way to say "potty training."

en=teacher
Correct. "如厕" is a more formal verb for using the restroom, and "训练" (xùn liàn) means training. And notice the grammar here: "难吗？". She is asking a general question about whether potty training is difficult in general, not if a specific instance was difficult.

en=student
So if she said "难吗", she is asking "is it difficult?" But if she said "难了吗?", she would be asking "did it become difficult?".

en=teacher
You are catching on quickly! The distinction is key. Finally, the nanny responds.

zh=2
非常困难，但是没关系。
It is very difficult, but it is okay.

en=student
"非常困难" (fēi cháng kùn nán). "非常" is very, and "困难" is difficult. And "没关系" (méi guān xi) is the classic "it doesn't matter" or "it's okay."

en=teacher
Exactly. Even when things are messy, we say "没关系."

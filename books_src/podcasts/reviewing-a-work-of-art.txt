en=teacher
Exactly! And that's where our grammar comes in. Today, we are focusing on two essential structures. First, the use of 是 to connect nouns, which allows you to define things. In the form, A is B. For example, 这是一本书 (This is a book) or 我是老师 (I am a teacher). We will also use the negative form, 不是, to say "is not."

en=student
Right, so if I'm looking at a banana taped to a wall and I'm skeptical, I can say 这不是艺术 (This is not art).

en=teacher
Perfect. Second, we are looking at expressing "excessive" or highly emotional states using the structure 太 plus an adjective, followed by 了. It’s not just "very"; it carries an emotional weight, like "too" or "so." For example, 太好了 (That's great!) or 太贵了 (It's too expensive!).

en=student
So, if something is just "very" strange, I could say 很难看 (hěn nán kàn), but if it's "too" weird to even process, I'd use 太奇怪了 (tài qíguài le).

en=teacher
Spot on. In our dialogue today, you will hear a heated exchange at a modern art exhibition. You'll meet a visitor who is very confused by a particular piece of art, and a highly dramatic art critic who thinks the visitor is missing the entire point.

en=student

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's break down that intense conversation between our visitor and our critic. We start with the visitor, speaker one.

zh=1
这幅画太奇怪了。这真的是艺术吗？
This painting is too strange. Is this really art?

en=student
"这幅画" (zhì fú huà) means "this painting." "画" is the key word for painting or to draw. And then we have our first grammar point: "太奇怪了" (tài qíguài le). "奇怪" means strange. As we discussed, using "太...了" adds that sense of "it's too much" or "it's so...!"

en=teacher
Exactly. And then he asks, "这真的是艺术吗？" "真的" (zhēn de) means "really" or "truly." He's questioning the very definition of the object.

zh=2
这不是奇怪，这是艺术！这是灵魂的颜色。
This is not strange, this is art! This is the color of the soul.

en=teacher
Speaker two, the critic, jumps in immediately with "这不是奇怪" (zhè bú shì qíguài). Here, we see the negative form of our first grammar point. She is saying "This is not strange."

en=student
Wait, Ellen, in the phrase "这不是奇怪," why is she using an adjective like "strange" after "is not"? Isn't "is" usually for nouns?

en=teacher
That's a great observation, Ryan. In Chinese, "是" can be used to link a subject to a noun, but in common speech, it is often used to emphasize a quality or to say "the thing is [adjective]." It's a way of saying "The reality is [adjective]." She's correcting his perception.

en=student
I see! It's like saying, "The situation isn't 'strange,' it's 'art'." And then she follows it up with "这是灵魂的颜色" (zhè shì línghún de yánsè).

en=teacher
Yes. "灵魂" (línghún) is soul, and "颜色" (yánsè) is color. Here, she is using the classic "A 是 B" structure: This [subject] is [color of the soul].

zh=1
但是这只是一个红色的圆圈。这太简单了！
But this is just a red circle. This is too simple!

en=teacher
The visitor is not convinced. "这只是一个红色的圆圈" (zhè zhǐshì yīgè hóngsè de yuánquān). "只是" (zhǐshì) means "is only" or "just." And then, "这太简单了" (zhè tài jiǎndān le). "简单" means simple. He thinks the work lacks complexity.

zh=2
简单才是真正的美！你太不懂艺术了。
Simplicity is true beauty! You understand art too little!

en=student
The critic is getting heated! "简单才是真正的美" (jiǎndān cái shì zhēnzhèng de měi). "才是" (cái shì) adds emphasis, like "is indeed" or "is what truly is."

en=teacher
And then the blow: "你太不懂艺术了" (nǐ tài bù dǒng yìshù le). This is a heavy one. "不懂" (bù dǒng) means "to not understand." When she says you "too much" (太) "not understand" (不懂), she's basically saying you are totally ignorant about art.

en=student
Ouch. That's a harsh critique!

zh=1
我觉得这太贵了，这根本不是艺术。
I think this is too expensive; this is simply not art.

en=teacher
The visitor gives his final verdict: "我觉得这太贵了" (wǒ juéde zhè tài guì le). "贵" means expensive. He's moved from aesthetic criticism to financial criticism!

en=student
And then he ends with "这根本不是艺术" (zhè gēnběn bú shì yìshù). I love the use of "根本" (gēnběn) there. It's like "at all" or "simply." So "This is simply not art."

en=teacher
It’s a very strong way to negate something. "根本不是" is a fantastic phrase to use when you want to be firm in your disagreement.

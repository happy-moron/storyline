I love art, but sometimes I find it hard to describe what I see. I usually just say "it is good" or "it is bad."

en=teacher
That is why today's lesson is so important. We are going to learn how to be more specific using two grammar points. First, how to indicate a number in excess using 多. 

en=student
Is that like saying "more than" or "over"?

en=teacher
Exactly. We use it in two ways. If you are using a round number, like ten or fifty, you put 多 before the measure word. For example, 十多个人, which means more than ten people. 

en=student
And if the number is not a round number? Like, if I want to say "two and a bit hours"?

en=teacher
Great question! In that case, you put 多 after the measure word. For example, 两个多小时. So, the position changes depending on whether the number ends in zero or not.

en=student
That is a very helpful distinction. What is the second grammar point?

en=teacher
The second is using 几 to mean "several" or "a few." You use it with a measure word and a noun. For example, 几本书, which means several books.

en=student
Got it. So 几 is like "a few" when it is used this way. 

en=teacher
Precisely. Before we listen to our dialogue, let's look at our key vocabulary. 

en=teacher
We have 作品, which is a fourth tone and a fourth tone, meaning a work of art... 作品.

en=teacher
We have 杰作, which is a second tone and a fourth tone, meaning a masterpiece... 杰作.

en=teacher
We have 奇怪, which is a second tone and a fourth tone, meaning strange... 奇怪.

en=teacher
We have 无聊, which is a second tone and a second tone, meaning boring... 无聊.

en=student
Sounds good. What is the dialogue about?

en=teacher
In this scene, you will hear two friends, Li Wei and Xiao Mei, at a modern art gallery. Li Wei is a very enthusiastic art lover, while Xiao Mei is... well, she is a bit skeptical about this particular exhibition.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's break down this dialogue line by line. We start with Li Wei, who is clearly impressed.

zh=1
看看这个作品！简直是杰作！
Look at this work of art! It is simply a masterpiece!

en=student
"作品" means work of art, and "杰作" means masterpiece. Li Wei is being quite dramatic!

en=teacher
Yes, he is. Now, let's look at Xiao Mei's skeptical response.

zh=2
我觉得这只是几块奇怪的石头。
I think this is just several strange stones.

en=student
Wait, here is our grammar point! She said "几块". "几" means several, and "块" is the measure word for stones or pieces. 

en=teacher
Correct. And "奇怪" means strange. She is really not feeling the art.

en=teacher
Now, let's look at Li Wei's reaction to her lack of emotion.

zh=1
你怎么这么没感觉？这位艺术家已经有五十多岁了，他的作品非常有名。
How can you be so indifferent? This artist is already over fifty years old, and his works are very famous.

en=student
"你怎么这么..." is a common way to say "How can you be so..." or "Why are you so...". But I'm curious about "五十多岁". You mentioned that with round numbers, the "多" goes before the measure word?

en=teacher
Exactly. Since fifty is a round number, we say 五十多岁. It means over fifty years old. If she said she was twenty-one, she would say 二十一岁多.

en=student
That makes sense. It depends on whether you are rounding the number or describing a specific amount.

en=teacher
You've got it. Now, Xiao Mei is losing patience.

zh=2

We have already looked for over two hours, and I still find it very boring.

en=student
Ah, here is the second way to use "多"! Since two is not a round number like ten or fifty, the "多" comes after the measure word, "个". So, "两个多小时".

en=teacher
Perfect. And "无聊" means boring. She is definitely ready to leave.

en=teacher
Let's look at Li Wei's final attempt to convince her.

zh=1
你看，这幅画里有几种不同的感觉。
Look, there are several different feelings in this painting.

en=student
"几种" means several kinds. Here, "几" is used with the measure word "种" to mean "several kinds."

en=teacher
And finally, Xiao Mei's final dismissal.

zh=2
可是在我眼里，这只是几张没意思的纸。
But in my eyes, these are just several uninteresting pieces of paper.

en=student
"几张". "张" is the measure word for flat things, like paper. So "几张" is several sheets. And "没意思" is a great way to say "uninteresting" or "not interesting."

en=teacher
Exactly. She is definitely not an art critic!

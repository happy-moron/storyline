Exactly. When you are dealing with art, you need to talk about prices and quantities precisely. Today, we are covering two major grammar points. First, counting money. In Chinese, for everyday conversation, we use 块 for "yuan." When you have decimals, like fifty cents, you use 毛 or 角. For example, if something is fifteen yuan and fifty cents, you can say 一块五毛 or simply 一块五. 

en=student
So it is like saying "one buck fifty" in English. And the more formal way uses 元 instead of 块?

en=teacher
Precisely. Second, we are looking at how to ask about quantities using 几. To ask "how many" or "how much," the structure is 几 plus a measure word, then the noun. For example, if you want to ask "how many paintings," you would say 几幅画. You can't just say "how many paintings" without that middle measure word.

"看" means to look. And "艺术品" is our vocabulary word for artwork. I noticed she said "多美啊". Is that how you say "so beautiful"?

en=student
"艺术感" is "sense of art." Interesting. And she used "幅" (fú) there. Is that the measure word for paintings?

en=teacher
Exactly. Every noun in Chinese needs a measure word. For paintings, we use "幅".

zh=1
这幅画多少钱？
How much is this painting?

en=student
"多少钱" is the standard way to ask "how much money?" But I remember you said "几" can also be used for quantity. What is the difference?

en=teacher
Great question. We use "多少" (duōshao) when the number could be large or when we don't know the scale. We use "几" (jǐ) when we expect the number to be small, usually under ten or twelve. Since Xiao Mei doesn't know if this painting is a ten-yuan print or a ten-thousand-yuan masterpiece, "多少" is the safer bet.

zh=2
这幅画两千三百四十五块。
This painting is two thousand three hundred forty-five yuan.

en=student
Okay, let's look at that number. "两千三百四十五块". I have a question about the number two. Why didn't he say "二千"?

en=teacher
That is a very important distinction for learners! When you are counting, you use "二" (èr). But when you are stating a quantity, especially for large numbers like two thousand, two hundred, or two million, we use "两" (liǎng). So it is "两千", not "二千".

en=student
That makes sense. Two thousand, three hundred, forty-five. "两千三百四十五". And "块" is the colloquial way to say "yuan".

zh=1
天哪，太贵了！那这组画有几幅？
Oh my god, it is too expensive! So, how many paintings are in this set?

en=student
She says "太贵了". "太...了" is that classic "too [adjective]" structure. And then she asks "有几幅?". There's the "几 + measure word" grammar again!

en=teacher
Correct. "几幅" (jǐ fú) means "how many [paintings]."

zh=2
这组一共五幅画。

en=student
"一共" means "altogether" or "in total." That's very useful for shopping.

zh=1

en=student
Wow, eleven thousand seven hundred! That's "一万一千七百". 

en=teacher
Yes! "一万" is ten thousand. So "一万一千七百" is ten thousand, one thousand, seven hundred. It's a big number! And notice he ends with "块钱" (kuài qián). Adding "钱" after the amount is a very natural way to emphasize we are talking about money.

en=student

en=teacher
They do! "块" is often used alone in speech, but adding "钱" is very common.

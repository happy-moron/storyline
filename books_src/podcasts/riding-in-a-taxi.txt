Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are exploring the nuances of ability and possibility. Specifically, we are looking at the difference between two words that both translate to "can" in English: 会 and 能.

en=student
Right, this one always trips me up. If I want to say I can speak Chinese, do I say 我会说中文 or 我能说中文? It sounds like I'm trying to say the same thing twice!

en=teacher
That is a very common mistake! The key is the *source* of the ability. We use 会 to talk about a learned skill—something you had to study or practice to acquire. For example, you use 会 to say you know how to drive, how to swim, or how to speak a language. It's a "learned" can.

en=student
Okay, so "I learned how to do it." Got it.

en=teacher
Exactly. Now, 能 is used for physical ability or situational possibility. It’s about whether you are *able* to do something right now, given the circumstances or your physical state. For example, if you are too tired to walk, you would say 我不能走路. It’s not that you never learned how to walk, it's just that you can't do it at this moment.

en=student
So, if I say "I can drive," and I mean I possess the skill of driving, I use 会. But if I say "I can't drive today because my car is broken," I use 能.

en=teacher

en=student
"师傅" (sī jī) is a very common way to address a taxi driver or a craftsman. And here, she uses 能. She's asking, "Is it possible for you to drive faster right now?" It's about the possibility of the action occurring.

en=teacher
Exactly. And she says "我很着急" (wǒ hěn zháo jí). As we learned, 着急 means to be in a hurry or anxious. You can feel the stress in her voice.

zh=2

I know how to drive, but there are too many cars on the road now, I can't drive too fast.

en=student
This is the perfect example of the difference! He says "我会开车" (wǒ huì kāi chē). He is asserting his skill. He's basically saying, "I have the learned skill of driving."

en=teacher
But then he says "我不能开太快" (wǒ bù néng kāi tài kuài). He's not saying he lacks the skill to drive fast, he's saying the current situation—the heavy traffic—makes it impossible or not permitted to do so.

zh=1
那你能走那条小路吗？
Then can you take that side road?

en=student
She's looking for a solution. She asks "你能走...吗?" She is asking about the possibility of taking a specific route.

zh=2
那条路太窄了，我不会在那样的路开车。
That road is too narrow, I don't know how to drive on roads like that.

en=teacher
This is a very interesting use of 会. Here, the driver says "我不会在那样的路开车." He is saying that driving in extremely narrow, difficult spaces is a specific skill he doesn't possess. He's saying, "I don't know how to handle that type of driving environment."

en=student
So, if it's a skill of maneuvering or a specific technique, we use 会. That makes sense.

zh=1
没关系，求你了！我真的不能迟到。
It's okay, I am begging you! I really cannot be late.

en=student
"求你了" (qiú nǐ le) is quite dramatic! "I'm begging you." And then she finishes with "我真的不能迟到" (wǒ zhēn de bù néng chí dào).

en=teacher
Right. "迟到" means to be late. She uses 能 here because being late is a consequence of the situation. She is saying, "It is not possible for me to be late, or the circumstances won't allow me to be late." She's desperate.

en=student
So, just to recap: 会 is the "I studied for this" can, and 能 is the "it's possible/I'm able to" can.

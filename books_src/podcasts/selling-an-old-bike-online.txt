Exactly. And to do that well, you need to master two very important grammar points. First, the 是⋯⋯ 的 construction. This isn't used to say "something is something." Instead, it is used to emphasize specific details of an event that has already happened, such as when, where, or how it occurred. For example, if someone asks when you bought a car, you wouldn't just say "I bought it last year." You would say 我是去年买的 to emphasize that *last year* was indeed the time.

en=student
Ah, so it adds that extra layer of "it was [this specific thing] that happened." It focuses the attention on the detail.

en=teacher
Spot on. Second, we are tackling the classic confusion: 不 vs 没. This is a hurdle for every learner. In general, 不 is used for adjectives, habits, or things in the present and future. For example, 不贵 (not expensive) or 我不吃肉 (I don't eat meat). 没, on the other hand, is used to negate past actions or to say something "has not" happened. For example, 我没买 (I didn't buy) or 我没有钱 (I don't have money).

en=student
So, if I want to say "It is not broken," I use 不, but if I want to say "I didn't fix it," I use 没?

en=teacher
You've got it! One is about a state or habit, and the other is about an action that didn't take place. Before we get to our dialogue, let's look at our key vocabulary.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's break down this conversation between the buyer and the seller. We start with the buyer checking availability.

zh=1
你好，请问这辆自行车还在吗？
Hello, excuse me, is this bicycle still available?

en=student
"请问" is a very polite way to say "may I ask." And "还在吗" uses "还" to mean "still." It's a standard way to check if something is still in stock.

en=teacher
Right. Then the buyer gets down to business by asking about the history of the bike.

zh=1
我想买。它是哪年买的？
I want to buy it. What year was it bought?

en=student
Here is our first grammar point! "它是哪年买的？" The buyer is asking "When was it bought?" using the 是⋯⋯ 的 structure. If they just said "它哪年买?" it would sound a bit incomplete or strange in this context. They are asking for the specific detail of the time.

en=teacher
Exactly. And listen to the seller's response.

zh=2
我是去年买的，就在这家店。
I bought it last year, right here at this shop.

en=student
The seller uses the structure twice! "我是去年买的" emphasizes *when* (last year), and "就在这家店" emphasizes *where* (at this shop). It sounds very definitive.

en=teacher
It does! Now, the buyer is getting suspicious about the condition.

zh=1
状况怎么样？坏了吗？
How is the condition? Is it broken?

en=student
"怎么样" is "how." And "坏了吗" uses "了" to indicate a state of change or a completed action—in this case, whether it *has become* broken.

en=teacher
The seller is trying to reassure the buyer.

zh=2
不坏。这辆车一点儿都不旧，也很干净。
It's not broken. This bike isn't old at all, and it is very clean.

en=student
"不坏" is a great example of "不" with an adjective. It's describing the current state. And "一点儿都不旧" is a very useful pattern. "一点儿都不..." means "not at all." It's like saying "not a little bit old."

en=teacher
Precisely. Now, the buyer asks about maintenance.

zh=1
你修过吗？
Have you repaired it?

en=student
The "过" at the end indicates an experience. "Have you ever had the experience of repairing it?"

en=teacher
And the seller's response is a perfect example of "没" for past actions and experiences.

zh=2
我从来没修过，因为我没怎么骑。
I have never repaired it, because I didn't ride it much.

en=student
"从来没...过" means "never have ever done something." It's a very common way to say you have no experience with something. And "我没怎么骑" uses "没" because they are negating the action of riding that happened in the past.

en=teacher
Right. If they said "我不骑," they would be saying "I don't ride (as a general rule/habit)." But since they are talking about their history with this specific bike, they must use "没." Finally, the price.

zh=1
价格呢？
What about the price?
zh=2
不贵，五百块。
Not expensive, five hundred yuan.

en=student
"不贵" is "not expensive." Again, using "不" because it's describing the current state or quality of the price. If the seller wanted to say "The price didn't go up," they would use "没," but for "It is not expensive," "不" is the only way to go.

en=teacher
You're getting the hang of it! The difference between describing a state with "不" and negating an action with "没" is the key to sounding natural.

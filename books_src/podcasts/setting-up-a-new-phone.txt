INTRO

en=teacher
Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are diving into the frustration of modern technology and the grammar needed to express it.

en=student
Oh boy, I can already feel the stress. Is this about losing a phone?

en=teacher
Not exactly, but it is about the chaos of setting up a new one. Today we are learning two very important adverbs: 更, which means "even more," and 真, which means "really" or "truly."

en=student
So, if something is already expensive, and I buy one that is even more expensive, I use 更?

en=teacher
Exactly. You place 更 directly before an adjective to show an increase in degree, usually in a comparison. For example, 更贵 means even more expensive.

en=student
And 真 is more for when you are just making an observation or an exclamation? Like, "This is really good!"?

en=teacher
Perfect. 真 is used to express a strong feeling about a quality. For example, 真漂亮 means really beautiful. You wouldn't use 真 to compare two things; you use it to react to something.

en=student
That makes sense. So, if I'm comparing two phones, I use 更. If I'm just looking at one phone and reacting to how pretty it is, I use 真.

en=teacher
You've got it. Before we listen to the dialogue, let's look at our six key vocabulary words.

en=teacher
First, 手机, (third tone and first tone), meaning mobile phone... 手机.

en=teacher
Second, 贵, (fourth tone), meaning expensive... 贵.

en=teacher
Third, 漂亮, (fourth tone and light tone), meaning beautiful... 漂亮.

en=teacher
Fourth, 复杂, (second tone and second tone), meaning complicated... 复杂.

en=teacher
Fifth, 好用, (third tone and fourth tone), meaning easy to use or useful... 好用.

en=teacher
Sixth, 简单, (second tone and simple tone), meaning simple... 简单.

en=student
Got it. So, what is the scenario?

en=teacher
In this episode, we are going to hear a conversation between two friends, Xiao Li and Dawei. Dawei has just bought a very expensive new phone, but he is having a terrible time setting it up and is already regretting the cost.

DIALOGUE

zh=1
这部手机比我之前的更贵！我真的快没钱了。
This phone is even more expensive than my previous one! I'm really running out of money.

zh=2
别抱怨了。你看，这个屏幕真漂亮。
Stop complaining. Look, this screen is really beautiful.

zh=1
漂亮有什么用？设置起来更复杂了！
What is the use of being beautiful? Setting it up is even more complicated!

zh=2
这个新系统真好用，你慢慢学。
This new system is really easy to use, just learn it slowly.

zh=1
我觉得以前的手机更简单。
I think my old phone was even simpler.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's break down this tense conversation. It starts with Dawei, speaker one, who is sounding quite stressed.

zh=1
这部手机比我之前的更贵！我真的快没钱了。
This phone is even more expensive than my previous one! I'm really running out of money.

en=student
"这部手机比我之前的更贵" (Zhè bù shǒujī bǐ wǒ zhīqián de gèng guì). We see our first grammar point here. He is comparing the new phone to his previous one using 比 (bǐ), and he says it is even more expensive using 更.

en=teacher
That's right. And then he says "我真的快没钱了" (Wǒ zhēn de kuài méi qián le). Here, 真 is used to emphasize that he is "really" almost out of money.

en=student
Quick question, Ellen. Can I say "我更没钱了" instead of "我真的没钱了"?

en=teacher
It's possible, but it changes the meaning. "我真没钱了" sounds like an exclamation of your current state, like "I'm really broke!" If you say "我更没钱了," it implies you were already broke, and now you are even more broke than before.

en=student
Ah, so 更 is for the comparison, and 真 is for the intensity of the feeling.

en=teacher
Exactly. Next, we have Xiao Li, speaker two, trying to be positive.

zh=2
别抱怨了。你看，这个屏幕真漂亮。
Stop complaining. Look, this screen is really beautiful.

en=student
"这个屏幕真漂亮" (Zhè ge píngmù zhēn piàoliang). "屏幕" is screen. And she uses 真 to emphasize how beautiful it is.

en=teacher
Indeed. But Dawei isn't having it.

zh=1
漂亮有什么用？设置起来更复杂了！
What is the use of being beautiful? Setting it up is even more complicated!

en=student
"设置" (shèzhì) means to set up or to configure. He says it's "更复杂" (gèng fùzá), even more complicated.

en=teacher
He is clearly frustrated. He thinks the beauty of the phone doesn't make up for the difficulty of using it.

en=student
Right. And then Xiao Li tries again.

zh=2
这个新系统真好用，你慢慢学。
This new system is really easy to use, just learn it slowly.

en=teacher
"好用" (hǎoyòng) is such a useful word. It literally means "good to use." And she uses 真 to say it is "really" good to use.

en=student
And finally, Dawei gives up.

zh=1
我觉得以前的手机更简单。
I think my old phone was even simpler.

en=teacher
"简单" (jiǎndān) means simple. He's using 更 again because he is comparing the new phone's complexity to the old one's simplicity. He's basically saying the old one was even simpler.

en=student
So, to recap, if you are comparing two things and want to say "even more," you use 更. If you are just reacting to something with emotion, like "That's really cool!" or "That's really expensive!", you use 真.

en=teacher
Spot on, Ryan.

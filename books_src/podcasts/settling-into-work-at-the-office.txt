That's right. We are going to learn how to settle into a new workspace. We'll be focusing on two very important grammar points. The first is the adverb 也, which means "also" or "too." It is used right before a verb or an adjective.

en=student
I know the word 也, but I always get the order a little mixed up. Does it go at the end of the sentence like "also" in English? Like, "I am a student also"?

en=teacher
In Chinese, it must come before the verb or the adjective. So, instead of saying "I am a student also," you would say "I also am a student." For example, 我也学习 (I also study) or 我也很忙 (I am also busy).

en=student
Got it. Before the action or the description. And what is the second grammar point?

en=teacher
The second one is asking about the degree of something using 多. When you put 多 before an adjective, it turns it into a question like "how" much. For example, 多大 means "how big," and 多高 means "how tall."

en=student
So if I want to ask "How fast is the internet?", I would say 多快?

en=teacher
Exactly! Now, in our dialogue, you will hear a new employee arriving at her new job, meeting a colleague, and interacting with her manager. You will hear how to use 也 to talk about office equipment and how to use 多 to ask about the office setup. Let's listen.

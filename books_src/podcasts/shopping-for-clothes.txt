Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are diving into a little word that does a huge amount of heavy lifting in the Chinese language: 就, jiù.

en=student
Oh man, 就. I feel like I see that character everywhere, but I'm not always sure how to use it properly. It seems like it changes meaning depending on where it sits in the sentence.

en=teacher
You are absolutely right, Ryan. It is incredibly versatile. Today, we are focusing on two specific ways to use it. First, we are looking at how to express earliness or things happening sooner than expected. The structure is Subject, then the Time, then 就, then the Verb, followed by 了. For example, 我八点就来了 (I arrived as early as eight o'clock).

en=student
So it’s not just "I arrived at eight," it’s "I arrived *already* at eight," implying I was early?

en=teacher
Exactly! It adds that sense of "as early as" or "already." Second, we are looking at using 就 to mean "then" or to indicate a consequence. This is often used in an "if... then..." construction. For example, 如果下雨，我就不去了 (If it rains, then I won't go).

en=student
Okay, so one version is about being early, and the other is about what happens next as a result of something.

en=teacher
Spot on. Today’s theme is shopping for clothes, but it’s not a relaxing stroll through the mall. We are going to hear a bit of a stressful encounter. You'll hear a customer who is in a major rush, a shop assistant trying to make a sale, and a friend who is... well, a little bit late. Pay close attention to how the time pressure changes the way the customer uses 就.

"欢迎光临" is a classic. You hear it every time you walk into a shop in China. And "有什么可以帮您的吗?" is the standard "Can I help you?"

en=student
Wait, let's look at that second sentence: "我下午两点就要去参加聚会了." You used the grammar point we talked about! "Two o'clock" is the time, then 就, then the verb "to go to a party," and finally 了.

en=teacher
Exactly. By saying "两点就要...了", she isn't just stating the time; she is emphasizing that the event is coming up soon and she is on a strict schedule. It conveys that urgency.

en=student
And she also said "时间有点紧," which means "time is a bit tight." That makes sense given the context.

Yes, very observant! For dresses and skirts, we use "条" (tiáo). And "气质" (qìzhì) is a great word—it means temperament, manner, or aura.

en=student
So the "就" here links the condition of the discount to the action of buying.

en=teacher
Correct. Now the assistant tries to close the deal.

zh=1

If you buy two today, we will give you a ten percent discount.

en=student
Hold on, I always get confused by discounts in Chinese. "打九折" means ten percent off?

en=teacher
Yes! In Chinese, you say the amount you *do* pay. So "打九折" means you pay ninety percent of the price, which is a ten percent discount. If it was "打八折," that would be a twenty percent discount.

en=student
Got it. That is going to trip me up in a real store! Let's look at the customer's reaction.

zh=2
那太好了！那我这就去试衣间试一下。
That's great! Then I will go to the fitting room to try it on right now.

en=student
She says "那我就..." which is "Then I will..." and then she says "这就去". Does "这就" mean "immediately"?

en=teacher
Exactly. "这就" (zhè jiù) is a very common way to say "right now" or "immediately." It combines "this" and "then" to indicate the action is happening as a direct consequence of the news.

en=teacher
This last part is very important for your grammar studies. The customer asks, "你怎么才来?" (Nǐ zěnme cái lái?).

en=student
Wait, I thought we were learning about 就? Why did she use 才 (cái) here?

en=teacher
That is a brilliant question, Ryan. In Chinese, 就 (jiù) and 才 (cái) are often opposites when talking about time. While 就 implies something happened early or quickly, 才 implies something happened late or slowly.

en=student
So, if I say "他八点就来了," I'm saying he arrived early, at eight. But if I say "他八点才来," I'm saying he arrived late, even though it was eight?

en=teacher
Exactly! So when the customer says "你怎么才来?", she is expressing frustration because the friend arrived later than expected. She's saying, "How come you just *now* arrived?"

en=student
That makes so much sense. It really highlights the difference in tone. And "半个小时" is half an hour.

en=teacher
Yes. "半" (bàn) is half, and "个" (gè) is the measure word for hours.

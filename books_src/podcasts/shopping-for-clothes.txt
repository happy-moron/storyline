en=teacher
Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are going to learn how to talk about what we do and do not want, specifically when we are out shopping.

en=student
That sounds very useful, Ellen. I often find myself in shops in Beijing wanting to say "I don't want this," but I always get a bit nervous about the grammar.

en=teacher
Don't worry, Ryan! Today we are covering two very important points. First, the negation with "不." You use "不" followed by a verb or an adjective to express "not" in the present or future, or to describe a characteristic. For example, "我不喜欢" means "I don't like."

en=student
Right, like "我不大" or "我不吃." But wait, I've heard "没" used for negation too. Is "不" the same as "没"?

en=teacher
That is a great question! "没" is used to negate past actions or the verb "to have," while "不" is for habits, present states, or descriptions. Since we are shopping and talking about what we like right now, we will use "不."

en=student
Got it. And what about the other grammar point?

en=teacher
The second point is the adverb "都," which means "all" or "both." This is a very important word for natural-sounding Chinese. A key rule is that "都" must always be placed after the subject and before the verb or adjective. For example, instead of saying "All are big," you say "They all are big" — "他们 đều 大."

en=student
So, "these are all too big" would be "这些都太大了"?

en=teacher
Exactly! You've got it. Today's dialogue features two characters: a friendly shop assistant and a customer who is looking for some new clothes. They will use both "不" and "都" several times. Let's listen to their conversation.

en=teacher
Alright, let's dive into the dialogue. Let's listen to the first line again.
zh=1
欢迎光临！您好！您想看点什么？
Welcome! Hello! What would you like to look at?

en=teacher
Here, the assistant uses "您" (nín), which is the polite form of "you." And "想看" (xiǎng kàn) means "want to look/see."

en=student
The customer responds simply.
zh=2
你好。我想看看这些衣服。
Hello. I would like to look at these clothes.

en=teacher
"这些" (zhèxiē) means "these."

en=student
Then the assistant tries to make a sale!
zh=1
这些衣服都很漂亮，也非常便宜。
These clothes are all very beautiful, and also very cheap.

en=teacher
Notice the use of "都" here. "这些衣服都很漂亮." She is saying "These clothes are all very beautiful." Remember, "都" comes after the subject "these clothes" and before the adjective "beautiful." She also uses "非常" (fēicháng), which means "very" or "extremely."

en=student
And then comes the rejection!
zh=2
这些都太大了。我不喜欢这些。
These are all too big. I don't like these.

en=teacher
This line is perfect for our lesson. "这些都太大了" uses "都" to say "they are all too big." And "我不喜欢这些" uses "不" to negate the verb "喜欢" (to like).

en=student
Wait, Ellen, in "这些都太大了," why is there a "了" at the end?

en=teacher
Good eye! When using "太...了" (tài...le) to mean "too [adjective]," you almost always include that "了" at the end of the phrase. It's a very common pattern.

en=student
Okay, so "too big" is "太大了." Now let's look at the next part.
zh=1
那这件红色的怎么样？这件也是新的。
How about this red one? This one is also new.

en=teacher
"件" (jiàn) is the measure word for clothing. "红色" (hóngsè) is the color red.

en=student
But the customer is still not happy.
zh=2
所有的衣服都不太合适。我不想要它们。
All of the clothes are not very suitable. I don't want them.

en=teacher
This is a big one! "所有的衣服" (suǒyǒu de yīfú) means "all of the clothes." Then we have "都不太合适" (dōu bù tài héshì). We have "都" (all) followed by "不" (not) and then "太合适" (very suitable). So, "all are not very suitable."

en=student
So you can have "都" and "不" right next to each other?

en=teacher
Yes! "都" modifies the whole situation. You are saying "In the case of all these clothes, they are all not suitable." And finally, "我不想要它们" (wǒ bù xiǎng yào tāmen) means "I don't want them." "想要" means "to want."

en=student
And the shop assistant is very professional.
zh=1
没关系。我们还有其他的颜色。
It's okay. We still have other colors.

en=teacher
"没关系" (méi guānxi) is "it's okay" or "no problem." And "其他的" (qítā de) means "other" or "another."

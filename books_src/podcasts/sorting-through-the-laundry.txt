en=teacher
Exactly! And to help you manage that chaos, today we are learning two essential grammar points. First, we have the measure word 个. In Chinese, you cannot just say "one bag" or "one cup." You must use a measure word between the number and the noun. The structure is number, followed by 个, followed by the noun. For example, 一个袋子, which means one bag.

en=student
Okay, so it is like saying "a" in English, but it's mandatory in Chinese?

en=teacher
Precisely. You can use it for a wide variety of things. Second, we are looking at how to state the date. In English, we say "May twelfth," but in Chinese, we always go from the largest unit to the smallest: year, then month, then day. So, it follows the structure number, year, number, month, number, day.

en=student
So, instead of saying "May twelfth, two thousand twenty-four," I would say "two thousand twenty-four year, five month, twelve day"?

en=teacher
You've got it! It feels reversed at first, but it makes a lot of sense once you get used to it. Before we get into our dialogue, let's look at our key vocabulary.

Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are tackling a very practical, slightly messy theme: sorting through the laundry.

en=student
Laundry? That is definitely a part of real life, but I usually try to avoid talking about it in a foreign language!

en=teacher
Well, you will need these words when you are living in a Chinese-speaking household or staying with a host family. Today, we are focusing on two very important grammar points. First, we are looking at the universal measure word, 个, which is used in the structure: Number plus 个 plus Noun. You use this to count almost everything, from people to objects. For example, 一个学生, which means one student, or 一个杯子, which means one cup.

en=student
So it is like saying "one apple" or "two books" in English, but in Chinese, you cannot just say the number and the noun; you must have that middle measure word.

en=teacher
Exactly. If you skip the 个, it sounds very broken. Second, we are learning the structure of dates. In Chinese, dates always go from the largest unit to the smallest: Year, then Month, then Day. The pattern is: Number plus 年, Number plus 月, Number plus 日 or 号. For example, 二零二四年五月一号, which is May first, two thousand twenty-four.

en=student
That is a big difference from English. We go from small to large, like May first, but Chinese goes big to small. It makes a lot of sense when you think about it as a timeline.

en=teacher
It does! Before we jump into our dialogue, let's go over our six key vocabulary words.

en=teacher
First, we have 衣服, (first tone and second tone), meaning clothes... 衣服.

en=teacher
Next, we have 衬衫, (fourth tone and first tone), meaning shirt... 衬衫.

en=teacher
Then, we have 袋子, (fourth tone and second tone), meaning bag... 袋子.

en=teacher
We also have 杯子, (first tone and second tone), meaning cup... 杯子.

en=teacher
Then there is 脏, (first tone), meaning dirty... 脏.

en=teacher

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's look at that dialogue and break it down line by line. We start with Chen.

zh=1
你看！这里有三个脏袋子！
Look! There are three dirty bags here!

en=student
"你看" (nǐ kàn) is a very common way to say "Look!" or "See?". And "这里" (zhè lǐ) means here. But Ellen, I noticed he said "三个脏袋子". He used the grammar point we talked about!

en=teacher
Yes! "三个袋子" is the pattern: Number, three, then the measure word 个, and then the noun, bag. He added "脏" (zāng), which means dirty, right before the noun. So, "three dirty bags."

en=student
That makes sense. Then Li responds.

zh=2
没关系。袋子里有几件衣服？
It does not matter. How many clothes are in the bag?

en=teacher
"没关系" (méi guān xi) is a classic phrase meaning "it does not matter" or "no problem."

en=student
And she asks "袋子里" (dài zi lǐ). I know "袋子" is bag, so "lǐ" must mean "inside," right?

en=teacher
Spot on. And she uses "几件" (jǐ jiàn). "几" (jǐ) is used when asking "how many" for small numbers. Now, pay attention to "件". We are learning "个" today, but for clothing like "衣服" (yī fu), we usually use the measure word "件" (jiàn).

en=student
So "一个衬衫" might be understood, but "一件衬衫" is the proper way?

en=teacher
Yes, exactly. You're learning fast! Now let's look at Chen's reaction.

zh=1
有三个衬衫，还有一个杯子！
There are three shirts and one cup!

en=student
He is using "个" for the shirts again. Is that okay?

en=teacher
In a real conversation, if you said "三个衬衫" (sān gè chèn shān), people would understand you perfectly, but as we noted, "件" is more accurate for shirts. But he uses "一个杯子" (yī gè bēi zi) for "one cup," and since "杯子" uses "个," that is perfectly correct.

en=student
Okay, so the number, then the measure word, then the noun. Got it. Then they start talking about their trip.

zh=2
我们什么时候去旅行？

en=teacher
"什么时候" (shén me shí hou) is the standard way to ask "when." And "旅行" (lǚ xíng) is our vocabulary word for "to travel."

en=student
Then Chen gives the date.

zh=1

We are leaving on October twentieth.

en=teacher
Here we see our date structure. "十月" (shí yuè) is October, and "二十号" (èr shí hào) is the twentieth. As I mentioned, "号" (hào) is very common in spoken Chinese for the day, whereas "日" (rì) is more formal or used in writing.

en=student

en=teacher
"哎呀" (āi ya) is a great interjection for "oh no" or "oh my." "那" (nà) means "then." And "快点" (kuài diǎn) means "quickly." They have to "洗衣服" (xǐ yī fu) — wash clothes!

Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are tackling a situation that can happen to anyone, anywhere: a clumsy moment in a restaurant. Specifically, we are talking about what happens when you spill your food.

en=student
Oh no, that sounds like a nightmare. I cannot even think about spilling hot soup on myself!

en=teacher
It can be very embarrassing, but knowing how to describe the situation and ask questions will help you handle it calmly. Today, we are learning two key grammar points. First, how to ask about the state or quality of something using 怎么样 (zěn me yàng). For example, 你觉得这碗拉面怎么样？ (How do you think this bowl of ramen is?) or 天气怎么样？ (How is the weather?).

en=student
So, 怎么样 is like "how is it" or "how about it" when you want to know about the condition or quality of something?

en=teacher
Exactly. Second, we are looking at the placement of question words. In English, we often put the question word at the beginning, like "What happened?". But in Chinese, the question word usually stays in the exact same position where the answer would go. If the answer is "Something happened," then the question is "What happened?"—which becomes 发生什么事了？ (fā shēng shén me shì le). The "what" replaces the "something."

en=student
That makes sense. So, the question word basically just acts as a placeholder for the information you are missing.

en=teacher
Perfectly said. Before the dialogue, let's look at our six key vocabulary words.

en=teacher
We have 拉面, (first tone and fourth tone), meaning ramen... 拉面.

en=teacher
We have 洒, (third tone), meaning to spill... 洒.

en=teacher
We have 味道, (fourth tone and fourth tone), meaning taste or flavor... 味道.

en=teacher
We have 弄脏, (fourth tone and first tone), meaning to make dirty... 弄脏.

en=teacher
We have 服务员, (second tone, fourth tone, and second tone), meaning waiter or server... 服务员.

en=teacher
We have 没关系, (second tone, first tone, and fourth tone), meaning it's okay or no problem... 没关系。

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's look at that dialogue, and break it down line by line. We start with the customer, who is clearly having a bad time.

zh=1
哎呀！怎么会这样？我的拉面全洒了！
Oh no! How could this happen? My ramen is all spilled!

en=student
"怎么会这样" (zěn me huì zhè yàng) is such a natural way to express frustration. It's like saying "How could it be like this?" or "How did this happen?".

en=teacher
Yes, and he uses "洒" (sǎ), to spill, to describe the ramen.

zh=2
哎呀！发生什么事了？需要帮助吗？
Oh my! What happened? Do you need help?

en=student
Here is one of our grammar points! "发生什么事了" (fā shēng shén me shì le). The "什么" (shén me), meaning what, is placed right before the noun "事" (shì), which means matter or thing. So, "what thing happened."

en=teacher
Correct. It is much more direct than English. Then the customer responds.

zh=1
我的裤子被汤弄脏了，真是太倒霉了！
My pants were made dirty by the soup; I am so unlucky!

en=student
"弄脏" (nòng zāng) is a really useful word. It is the verb "to do" or "to make" combined with "dirty." So, "to make dirty."

en=teacher
Exactly. And "倒霉" (dǎo méi) means unlucky or having bad luck. Now, the waiter tries to be professional.

zh=2
别担心，没关系。这碗拉面的味道怎么样？
Don't worry, it's okay. How was the taste of this bowl of ramen?

en=student
"味道怎么样" (wèi dào zěn me yàng). There's that 怎么样 again! It's asking about the quality of the "味道" (wèi dào), the taste.

en=teacher
Right. And the customer's reaction is quite funny. He hasn't even eaten!

zh=1
我还没开始吃呢，味道看起来还可以，但是现在太糟糕了。
I haven't even started eating yet. The taste looks okay, but now it is too terrible.

en=student
He says "看起来还可以" (kàn qǐ lái hái kě yǐ), meaning "it looks okay" or "it looks alright." But then he uses "糟糕" (zāo gāo), which means terrible or a mess.

en=teacher
The waiter takes the blame and offers a solution.

zh=2
对不起，是我的错。我马上给你拿一碗新的。
I am sorry, it is my fault. I will go get you a new bowl immediately.

en=student
"一碗新的" (yī wǎn xīn de). "碗" is the measure word for a bowl. He's asking for "one new bowl."

en=teacher
Then she asks this important question.

zh=2
你觉得什么时候方便吃？
When do you think is a convenient time to eat?

en=student
"什么时候" (shén me shí hou) means "when." Just like our "what" example, it sits in the position of the answer. Instead of saying "at five o'clock is convenient," you say "when is convenient."

en=teacher
And the customer is just ready to move on from this disaster.

zh=1
没关系，你快点就好。
It's okay, just be quick.

en=student
"快点" (kuài diǎn) means "a bit faster" or "hurry up."

en=teacher
It's a very practical phrase to know when you're in a rush!

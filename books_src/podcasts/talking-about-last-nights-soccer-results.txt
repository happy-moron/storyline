en=teacher
Okay Mark, let's break this down line by line. The dialogue starts with Xiao Wang asking Li Na:

zh=1
昨晚的足球比赛你看了吗？
Did you watch last night's soccer match?

en=teacher
Here we have 看了 (kàn le) with the 了 indicating a completed action - "watched." But this isn't our grammar point today, just a different use of 了! Now Li Na responds:

zh=2
没看，我现在不看足球了。结果怎么样？
I didn't watch it. I don't watch soccer anymore. How did it end?

en=teacher
And here's our first target structure! 不看足球了 (bù kàn zúqiú le) - "don't watch soccer anymore." The pattern is 不 + Verb + Object + 了. Notice the 了 at the end changes the meaning from "I don't watch" to "I don't watch anymore" - implying she used to watch but stopped.

en=student
Interesting! So if I say 我不喝茶了, that means "I don't drink tea anymore"?

Perfect! 我不喝茶了 means you used to drink tea but you've stopped. Now, when Xiao Wang says:

zh=1
太精彩了！我们队终于赢了！
It was so exciting! Our team finally won!

en=teacher
We see 赢了 (yíng le) - another completed action 了, but also 终于 (zhōngyú) adds that sense of "finally" - this was a long-awaited win! Then Li Na asks:

zh=2
真的？比分是多少？
Really? What was the score?

en=teacher
比分 (bǐfēn) - score. This is a compound word made of 比 (bǐ - compare) and 分 (fēn - points). So literally "compared points" - the score!

en=student
Ah, so 分 means points, like in 得分 (défēn) - "to score points"!

en=teacher
Exactly, Mark! And 得分 (défēn) is a verb-object compound - 得 (dé) means "to get" and 分 (fēn) means "points." Now Xiao Wang gives the score:

zh=1
三比二。他们上半场本来落后，但是下半场进了两个球。
3 to 2. They were losing in the first half, but they scored two goals in the second half.

en=teacher
Here we have 进了两个球 (jìn le liǎng ge qiú) - "scored two goals." 进球 (jìnqiú) is a noun meaning "goal" the scored point, and here 进了 is the verb 进 (jìn - to enter) with 了. The whole phrase means "entered two goals" - which is how Chinese says "scored two goals." Also notice 上半场 (shàngbànchǎng) - first half, and 下半场 (xiàbànchǎng) - second half.

You got it! 球场 (qiúchǎng) is the field, and 一场比赛 (yī chǎng bǐsài) is "a match." Now Li Na asks about the goalkeeper:

zh=2
守门员表现怎么样？
How did the goalkeeper perform?

en=teacher
守门员 (shǒuményuán) - goalkeeper. This is a great compound: 守 (shǒu - to guard) + 门 (mén - door/gate) + 员 (yuán - person/member). So literally "door-guarding-person" - a goalkeeper! And 球门 (qiúmén) is the goal net itself - "ball door."

en=student
That's logical! And 球员 (qiúyuán) is "ball person" - a player!

en=teacher
Exactly! Now Xiao Wang answers:

zh=1

He wasn't great at the beginning, but he played better later.

Here we have 好了 (hǎo le) - another "new situation" 了! 表现好了 means "played better now" - a change from earlier in the game. This is the second target structure - putting 了 at the end of a sentence to indicate a new state: 表现好 (plays well) becomes 表现好了 (plays well now / has become good). Then Li Na asks:

zh=2
那裁判呢？有没有问题？
What about the referee? Were there any problems?

en=teacher
裁判 (cáipàn) - referee. And Xiao Wang responds:

en=teacher
得了红牌 (dé le hóngpái) - "got a red card." 得 (dé) is "to get" and here we have the completed action with 了. Red card - you might see this in soccer! Then Li Na asks:

zh=2
啊！谁输了？
Ah! Who lost?

en=teacher
输 (shū) - to lose. And Xiao Wang answers:

zh=1
客队输了。不过比赛平了八十分钟，最后才分出胜负。
The away team lost. But the match was tied for eighty minutes, and only at the end did they determine the winner.

en=teacher
Here we have TWO of our target structures! 客队输了 (kèduì shū le) - "the away team lost" - a new situation, they are now in a losing state. And 比赛平了八十分钟 (bǐsài píng le bāshí fēnzhōng) - "the match was tied for eighty minutes." Here 平 (píng) means "to tie" or "draw," and with 了 it's a completed state - it was tied for that duration. 平了 (píng le) can also mean "it's now tied" when used alone.

en=student
So 平了 can mean either "now it's tied" or "was tied for a period"?

en=teacher
Yes! Context tells you the difference. Finally, Li Na says:

zh=2
好可惜我没看。我现在又开始看足球了！
Too bad I missed it. I'm starting to watch soccer again now!

en=teacher
And here's our other structure again! 又开始看足球了 (yòu kāishǐ kàn zúqiú le) - "starting to watch again now." The 了 at the end signals a new situation - she has changed her mind and is now going to watch soccer again! Notice the word 又 (yòu) which means "again" - it emphasizes that she's returning to an old habit.

en=student
So the "new situation" 了 is like a status change marker - "now it's X." And the "not anymore" 了 is like a status change into a negative state. They're actually the same structure, just with a negative verb!

en=teacher
That's a brilliant observation, Mark! You're right - both structures use 了 to mark a change of state. With a negative verb, it means "now it's NOT this anymore." With a positive verb or adjective, it means "now it IS this." That's the beauty of the "new situation" 了 - it's all about signaling that something has changed!

Exactly. We are going to look at two structures. First, the basic one: using 点, meaning "o'clock," to state the hour. For example, 三点 (three o'clock). Second, the more detailed version: Number plus 点, plus another number, plus 分, meaning "minutes." For example, 三点十分 (three ten).

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's look at that dialogue, and break it down line by line. We start with Da Wei.

zh=1
你怎么还没到？表演七点十分就开始了。
Why haven't you arrived yet? The performance starts at seven ten.

en=student
"你怎么还没到?" That's a powerful way to ask why someone isn't here. "怎么" means "how" or "why," and "还没" means "not yet." And "到" means "to arrive."

en=teacher
Correct. Then he mentions the time: "表演七点十分就开始了." "表演" is the performance. Here, we see our grammar point: 七点 + 十分. Seven o'clock plus ten minutes. The "了" at the end of the sentence indicates that the event is about to happen or has reached a state.

zh=2
对不起，我迟到了。现在七点二十。
Sorry, I am late. It is seven twenty.

en=student
Xiao Ming apologizes. "对不起" is the standard "I am sorry." But then he says "我迟到了." Is "迟到" always used with "了" like that?

en=teacher
In this context, yes, because "迟到" is a change of state—he has *become* late. Now, look at the time: "现在七点二十." Here, he uses the basic structure: Number + 点 + Number. He didn't say "分" at the end. Is that okay?

en=student
Can you just skip the "分" when you are speaking casually?

en=teacher
Yes! In spoken Chinese, when you are just stating the time, it is very common to omit the "分" if the minutes are a multiple of five or just for brevity. Saying "七点二十" is much more natural than "七点二十分" in a casual setting.

zh=1
快点！这个喜剧演员的笑话非常有趣，我快笑死了。
Hurry up! This comedian's jokes are very interesting, I am dying of laughter.

en=student
"快点" is "hurry up." And then he says "笑话非常有趣." I know "笑话" is "joke" and "有趣" is "funny/interesting." But what is "笑死了"?

en=teacher
That is a great observation! "死了" literally means "to die," but when you put it after an adjective or a verb like "笑," it functions like "extremely" or "to death." So, "笑死了" means "laughing to death" or "dying of laughter." It's very common in informal speech!

en=student
"I'm dying of laughter." That's a very dramatic way to put it! I love it.

zh=2
没关系，我现在到了。我也准备好要大笑一场了。
It's okay, I am here now. I am also ready to have a good laugh.

en=student
"没关系" is "it's okay" or "no problem." And he says "我也准备好了." He's using "准备" which we learned, meaning "to prepare" or "to be ready."

en=teacher
Exactly. He's using "准备好" which means "to be ready." Then he says "大笑一场." "大笑" means to laugh loudly or a big laugh. It’s like saying "to have a good laugh."

zh=1
哈哈，你真好笑。
Haha, you are so funny.

en=student
And finally, "你真好笑." We know "笑" is to laugh, but "好笑" is "funny," right?

en=teacher
Yes! Adding "好" before a verb often turns it into an adjective. "好笑" is "funny" or "amusing." So "你真好笑" means "You are so funny."

en=student
So, to recap the grammar, for hours and minutes, I use "点" for the hour and "分" for the minutes. For example, "十点十分."

en=teacher
Precisely. And remember, in casual talk, you can often drop the "分."

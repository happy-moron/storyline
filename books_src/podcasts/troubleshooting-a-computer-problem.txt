Exactly. And to help you navigate these tech headaches, we are learning two very useful grammar points. First, we are looking at how to moderate adjectives using 还. When you place 还 before an adjective, it can mean "fairly," "quite," or "relatively." For example, 还可以 (fairly okay) or 还不错 (not bad/pretty good). It is a way to say something is acceptable but not necessarily amazing.

en=student
So, instead of saying something is "very good," I can say it is "fairly good" to sound a bit more nuanced?

en=teacher
Precisely. Second, we are mastering the use of a phrase to modify a noun using the particle 的. The structure is: Phrase plus 的, followed by the noun. For example, 我买的电脑 (the computer I bought) or 昨天买的手机 (the phone bought yesterday). This allows you to turn entire actions or descriptions into descriptors for your nouns.

en=student
That makes sense. It's like adding a descriptive tag to the object.

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's break down that dialogue line by line. We start with Li Na's frustration.

zh=1
我的电脑坏了！太慢了！
My computer is broken! It is too slow!

en=student
She's not happy! "我的电脑" is "my computer." And "坏了" means "broken" or "it broke."

en=teacher
Right. And "太慢了" uses the "太...了" structure to mean "too slow." The "了" here indicates a change of state—it wasn't slow before, but now it is.

zh=2
别着急。是刚才坏掉的系统吗？
Don't worry. Is it the system that just broke?

en=student
"别着急" is a great one to know; it means "don't worry" or "don't be anxious." But I want to look at "刚才坏掉的系统." That's a long phrase before the noun!

en=teacher
Yes! This is our second grammar point: Phrase + 的 + Noun. The phrase here is "刚才坏掉的," which means "that just broke." "刚才" means "just now," and "坏掉" means "to break." When you add "的," the whole thing "刚才坏掉的" becomes an adjective describing the "系统," which is "system."

zh=1
我不知道。这是我昨天买的电脑。
I don't know. This is the computer I bought yesterday.

en=student
Another one! "我昨天买的电脑." "我昨天买" means "I bought yesterday." So, "The computer I bought yesterday."

en=teacher
Perfect. You are picking this up quickly! It's just like in English. You are turning the clause "I bought yesterday" into a description for the noun "computer."

zh=2
让我看看。系统还可以吗？
Let me see. Is the system quite okay?

en=student
Here comes our first grammar point again. "还可以吗?" We know "可以" means "can" or "okay," but with "还" in front of it, it becomes "fairly okay" or "quite alright."

en=teacher
Exactly. If she said "系统很好吗?", she would be asking "Is the system very good?" But by using "还可以," she is asking if it is "fairly okay" or "decent." It’s much more common in daily conversation to use these moderated adjectives.

zh=1

It's okay, but it is too slow.

en=student
She says "还行" (hái xíng). Is that the same as "还可以"?

en=teacher
Very similar! "还行" and "还可以" are both used to mean "fairly okay" or "it's alright." They are interchangeable in many contexts. However, "太慢了" brings us back to that "too..." structure.

zh=2

It's no problem, we can solve it.

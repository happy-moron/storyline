INTRO

en=teacher
Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am James, and today we are exploring a very common, yet sometimes confusing, part of the Chinese language: the word 给, which means "to give."

en=student
I've seen this word everywhere, James. But I get confused because sometimes it acts like a verb, and sometimes it seems to act like a preposition.

en=teacher
That is exactly what we are tackling today! We are going to look at two specific structures. The first is when 给 acts as a preposition meaning "for" or "to" someone. The structure is Subject, plus 给, plus the Target, plus a Verb. For example, 我给你打电话, which means I call you. Here, "call" is the action being done for you.

en=student
Okay, so it’s "Subject + for someone + do something."

en=teacher
Precisely. The second structure is when 给 acts as a verb that takes two objects. This is when you actually give someone something. The structure is Subject, plus Verb, plus the Indirect Object, which is the person, plus the Direct Object, which is the thing being given. For example, 我给你一本书, meaning I give you a book.

en=student
So in the first one, the action is the main event, and "give" just tells us who it's for. But in the second one, "give" is the actual action itself.

en=teacher
Spot on! You are a natural. Today's theme is visiting the doctor for a check-up. You will hear a patient visiting a clinic, interacting with a nurse and a doctor. We will use vocabulary related to health, such as 舒服 (comfortable or well), 检查 (to examine or check), and 建议 (advice or to suggest).

en=student
I'm ready. Let's see if I can spot the difference between the two "give" structures in the dialogue.

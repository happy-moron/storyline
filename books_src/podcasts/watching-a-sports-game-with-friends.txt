Welcome to Learning Chinese. I am Ellen, and today we are talking about something that brings people together—or sometimes drives them crazy—sports!

en=student

en=teacher
Exactly. And that is our theme for today: watching a sports game with friends. Because when people get passionate about a game, they start making a lot of comparisons and asking for favors.

en=student
Comparisons? You mean like, "This player is better than that player"?

en=teacher
Precisely. Our first grammar point is the basic comparison structure using 比. The structure is Noun one plus 比 plus Noun two plus an adjective. For example, 我比你高, which means I am taller than you.

en=student
Got it. And can I add something to the end to show how much better it is? Like, "much better" or "a little bit better"?

en=teacher
Yes! You can add 多了 for "much more" or 一点儿 for "a little bit." So, 我比你高多了 is "I am much taller than you."

en=student
That makes sense. Now, what about the second grammar point?

en=teacher
The second one is using 给 to mean "for." The structure is Subject plus 给 plus Recipient plus a Verb Phrase. Instead of just saying "give me a beer," you can say "buy a beer for me." In Chinese, we say 我给你买啤酒.

en=student
Wait, so it's not just "giving" something? Like, if I say "给我" (give me), that's one thing, but "给我买" is "buy for me"?

en=teacher
You've got it. The verb comes after the person. Now, in today's dialogue, you are going to hear three friends hanging out, watching a very intense football match. One is a super fan, one is a bit more relaxed, and one is mostly interested in the snacks!

BREAKDOWN

en=teacher
Let's look at that dialogue and break it down line by line. We start with our super fan, Speaker One.

zh=1
这一场比赛比上一场精彩多了！
This match is much more exciting than the last one!

en=student
"这一场比赛" means this match. "上一场" means the previous match. And then we have the comparison "比...精彩多了".

en=teacher
Right. "精彩" means wonderful or exciting. And remember that "多了" we talked about? It's placed after the adjective to show a big difference. 

en=student
So if it was only a little bit exciting, I would say "精彩一点儿"?

en=teacher
Perfect. Next, Speaker Two, our more skeptical friend, says:

zh=2
我觉得这个球员比昨天那个厉害。
I think this player is much more capable than the one yesterday.

en=student
"我觉得" means I feel or I think. "球员" is player. And "厉害" is such a useful word. It can mean awesome, formidable, or capable, right?

en=teacher
Exactly. It is a very common way to describe someone who is impressive. Here, she is comparing the player today to the one from yesterday using the 比 structure.

en=student
Then Speaker Three interrupts. He sounds hungry.

zh=3
别说了，你能给我买瓶啤酒吗？
Stop talking, can you buy me a beer?

en=teacher
"别说了" is a very natural way to say "stop talking" or "enough talk." And here we see our second grammar point: "给我买".

en=student
So, "给" is the recipient, "我" is me, and "买" is to buy. So "buy for me." 

en=teacher
Exactly. Now, a quick question for you, Ryan. Do you know the difference between "给我买" and "买给我"?

en=student
Is "买给我" more like "buy it to me"? Like the direction is toward me?

en=teacher
That is a good way to think about it. "给我买" focuses on the benefit of doing the action for someone, while "买给我" focuses more on the direction of the item. In a casual setting like this, "给我买" is extremely common.

en=student
Got it. And then our fan, Speaker One, replies:

zh=1
没问题，我正在给你们点外卖呢。
No problem, I am currently ordering takeout for you guys.

en=teacher
"没问题" is "no problem." "正在" indicates the action is happening right now. And "点外卖" is a very important modern phrase—it means to order takeout or delivery.

en=student
And he uses "给你们" which means "for you guys."

en=teacher
Correct. Then Speaker Two compares the food:

zh=2
这家店的菜比那家好吃。
The food at this place is tastier than that one.

en=student
"这家店" is this shop or this restaurant. "那家" is that one. And "好吃" means delicious or tasty.

en=teacher
And finally, Speaker Three gets even more demanding!

zh=3
太棒了！快给我点点薯条！
Awesome! Hurry and order some fries for me!

en=student
"太棒了" is awesome. "快" is hurry. And "点点" is a way to say "order a little bit of" or just to make the verb "order" sound softer and more casual.

en=teacher
Yes, repeating a verb like "点点" or "看看" often makes the tone more relaxed in spoken Chinese.

They thought it was a very good idea.
They hired a furnace and became goldsmiths.

But two small things hurt their business.
First, people did not like the gold that was mixed with copper.
Second, when the two older brothers sold something, they would leave little Gluck to watch the furnace.
They would go next door to the alehouse and spend the money on drinks.

So they melted all their gold but did not make enough money to buy more.
Finally, they only had one large drinking mug left.
Little Gluck's uncle had given it to him.
Gluck really liked this mug and would never give it away for anything, even though he only ever drank milk and water from it.

The mug looked very strange.
The handle was made of two twisted ropes of flowing golden hair.
The hair was made so well that it looked more like silk than metal.
These ropes of hair went down and joined a beard and whiskers made just as well.
This beard and whiskers went around a small, angry face, made of the reddest gold you can imagine.
The face was right on the front of the mug.
It had a pair of eyes that seemed to look around the whole mug.

When you drank from the mug, you could feel the eyes watching you closely.
Schwartz even said that one time, after drinking seventeen mugs of Rhenish wine from it, he saw the eyes wink!

When it was time to melt the mug to make spoons, it made poor little Gluck very sad.
But his brothers just laughed at him.
They threw the mug into the melting pot and walked unsteadily out to the alehouse.
As usual, they left him to pour the melted gold into bars when it was ready.
